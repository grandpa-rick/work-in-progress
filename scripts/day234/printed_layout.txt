The (s − 1)-adic valuation of Hikita’s ⋆-product: a
 block law, cumulant leads, and a box symmetry

      Abstract. Hikita introduced a two-parameter deformation ⋆ of the product of sym-
      metric functions. Writing eλ1 ⋆ · · · ⋆ eλℓ = ∑µ cλµ (s, t) eµ , we show that the (s − 1)-adic
      valuation of cλµ is ℓ(λ) − κ (λ, µ), where κ is the maximal number of blocks into which
      λ and µ split compatibly. Leading coefficients factor over block decompositions into
      connected pieces. For a full merge the connected piece is a connected-graph cumulant
      with edge weights tλi λ j − 1. It interpolates between partition-lattice Möbius values at
      t = 0 and weighted Cayley counts at t = 1, and a gauge-invariant ratio distinguishes
      it from Dołega’s
                  ˛     column cumulants for t ̸= 0. The coefficients are invariant, up to a
      power of s, under complementation in a box. All second-order leading coefficients are
      given in closed form, via a residue formula for Green polynomials at two-part classes.
      Keywords: Hikita star product, Hall–Littlewood functions, elementary symmetric
      functions, cumulants, valuations


1     Introduction
Hikita [9] defined a commutative, associative product ⋆ on symmetric functions over
Q(q, t) by transporting the ordinary product through the level-one polynomial repre-
sentation of the affine Hecke algebra; we always say Hikita’s ⋆. We write s := q−1 . At
s = 1, ⋆ is the ordinary product, and at s = 0 (after rescaling) it degenerates to a Hall–
Littlewood product (Remark 2.3). Di Francesco and Kedem [4, §8.3] observed that the
associated t-deformed graded characters lose Schur positivity at generic t, and positivity
of ⋆-structure constants fails in every natural basis we tried. Positivity is lost, but structure
is not. This abstract shows that the structure survives in the (s − 1)-adic filtration.
    We expand the structure constants of eλ⋆ := eλ1 ⋆ · · · ⋆ eλℓ = ∑µ cλµ (s, t) eµ around
s = 1. We start from Hikita’s formula for ek ⋆ (·) as a sum over k-subsets (Proposition 2.1).
In it, s enters only through an Euler operator, so the (s − 1) p Taylor piece of ek ⋆ (·) is a
differential operator of order p, which can glue at most p existing blocks of parts to a
new part. This bounds the valuation below; exactness is settled at t = 0, where eλ⋆ is a
modified Hall–Littlewood function and nothing cancels. Main results:

7     Open problems
    1. Valuation ≥ 3. Connected leads with ℓ ≥ 4, κ = 1. Hierarchy: v = 1 ↔ pn ↔
       one t-string (Lemma 3.9); v = 2 ↔ p x py ↔ two strings (Theorem 6.3). Does the
       three-string shuffle sum factor pairwise?

    2. Zero set at generic t. At t = −2 seven pairs with n = 6 have v > ℓ − κ; for
       coarsenings this is cancellation between histories, e.g. Lead(1,1,1),(3) = [3]t (2 + t).
       Describe the zero set of [(s − 1)ℓ−κ ]cλµ in t.

    3. Counting. A formula for N (λ, µ) in Theorem 3.4; for ℓ(λ) = 3, µ = (n − 1, 1),
       κ = 1 we observe Leadλµ (1) = 2n2 − 6n + 3, independent of λ (n ≤ 10; computed
       only).

    4. Positivity. Leads are not t-positive (Example 6.8), so no “t-count of minimal flows”
       can hold. Is there a signed or cancellation-free model?
