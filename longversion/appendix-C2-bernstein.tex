\begin{lemma}[Bernstein relations from the word]\label{lem:bernstein}
Let $\pi$ be any operator on $\mathbb{Q}(s,t)[x_1,\dots,x_m]$ with $\pi T_k=T_{k+1}\pi$ for $1\le k\le m-2$
(e.g.\ $f\mapsto f(x_2,\dots,x_m,sx_1)$, or the operator $\pi$ of \S2.1), and define $Y_i$ by the word in \S2.1. Then for $1\le i\le m-1$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] $T_iY_iT_i=tY_{i+1}$;
\item[(b)] $T_iY_i=Y_{i+1}T_i-(t-1)Y_{i+1}$ and $T_iY_{i+1}=Y_iT_i+(t-1)Y_{i+1}$; hence $[T_i,Y_i+Y_{i+1}]=0$ and $T_iY_iY_{i+1}=Y_{i+1}Y_iT_i$;
\item[(c)] $T_iY_j=Y_jT_i$ for $j\notin\{i,i+1\}$.
\end{enumerate}
If moreover the $Y_i$ commute, then $T_if(Y)=f(Y)T_i$ for every $f\in\mathbb{Q}(s,t)[y_1,\dots,y_m]^{S_m}$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
(a) The trailing $T_i^{-1}$ of $Y_i$ cancels against $T_i$, and $T_i$ on the left extends $T_{i-1}\cdots T_1$ to $T_i\cdots T_1$, so the result is $t\cdot Y_{i+1}$.
(b) Multiply (a) by $T_i^{-1}=t^{-1}T_i-(1-t^{-1})$ on the right, respectively on the left. Adding the two relations cancels the $(t-1)Y_{i+1}$ terms. Applying the first relation and then the second gives
$T_iY_iY_{i+1}=Y_{i+1}T_iY_{i+1}-(t-1)Y_{i+1}^2=Y_{i+1}Y_iT_i$.
(c) Case $j\ge i+2$: the braid and far commutation relations give $T_i\,T_{j-1}\cdots T_1=T_{j-1}\cdots T_1\,T_{i+1}$; then $T_{i+1}\pi=\pi T_i$, and $T_i$ commutes with $T_{m-1}^{-1},\dots,T_j^{-1}$.
Case $j\le i-1$: $T_i$ commutes with $T_{j-1},\dots,T_1$; then $T_i\pi=\pi T_{i-1}$; and the braid relation in the form $T_{i-1}T_i^{-1}T_{i-1}^{-1}=T_i^{-1}T_{i-1}^{-1}T_i$ gives $T_{i-1}\,T_{m-1}^{-1}\cdots T_j^{-1}=T_{m-1}^{-1}\cdots T_j^{-1}\,T_i$.
Finally, a symmetric $f$ is a polynomial in $y_i+y_{i+1}$, $y_iy_{i+1}$ and the $y_j$ with $j\ne i,i+1$. If the $Y$'s commute, $f(Y)$ is the same polynomial in the corresponding operators, each of which commutes with $T_i$ by (b) and (c).
\end{proof}
