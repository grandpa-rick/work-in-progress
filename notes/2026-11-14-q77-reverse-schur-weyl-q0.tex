\section{Setup}
$H=H_N(0)$ acts on $(k^n)^{\otimes N}$ by $v_w\cdot\pi_i=v_{ws_i}$ on ascents and $v_w\cdot\pi_i=v_w$ otherwise.
Let $A$ be the image of $H$, $S=\End_H=S_0(n,N)$ (owned by Deng--Yang / Jung--Oh), and $B=\End_S$. Then $A\subseteq B$.

\section{Theorem}
\begin{theorem}[\gr{proved, written}]\label{thm:main}
For $n\ge2$ and $N\ge2n+1$, $A\subsetneq B$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em,itemsep=2pt]
\item Let $h=\pb_{\wo[1,n-1]}\cdot\pb_{\wo[N-n+1,N-1]}$, where $\pb=1-\pi$. For any set $C$ of weights, $v\mapsto(v\cdot1_C)\cdot h$ commutes with $S$.
\item It is not in $A$ whenever $C$ separates two weights all of whose parts are $\ge2$.
\item For $n=2$ the witness is ``$(1-\pi_1)(1-\pi_{N-1})$ on the weight space with $N-2$ ones'', for every $N\ge5$.
\item Proof idea: call a word \emph{bad} if some letter $1,\dots,n$ is missing, or the first occurrences of $1,\dots,n$ are not in increasing order of position, or the last occurrences are not. Bad words are closed under the $0$-Hecke moves, so they span a submodule $Y$. Being good is a necessary condition for reaching prefix $=$ suffix $=12\cdots n$ (no equivalence is claimed). $Y$ is killed by $h$. Every cross-weight $S$-generator lands in $Y$. A local-idempotent argument shows the split operator is not in $A$.
\item The proof is short and needs only standard facts.
\item One-line picture: $h$ maps $V^{\otimes N}$ into $\det\otimes V^{\otimes(N-2n)}\otimes\det$, where $\det$ is the $q=0$ antisymmetriser line on $n$ positions. $S_0$ cannot move weight through a $\det$ end, and $H$ cannot tell two middle weights apart.
\end{itemize}

\section{Surprise}\label{sec:surprise}
The census hunch ``fails iff $N\ge2n+1$'' is wrong. $(3,6)$ also fails, by an exact $\ZZ$ certificate, and the mechanism there is different (non-split).
The witness there is $a\cdot1_\mu$ with $a=\pb_{\wo[1,2]}\cdot\pi_3\cdot\pb_{\wo[4,5]}$ and $\mu=(1,2,3)$ or $\mu=(1,3,2)$.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em,itemsep=2pt]
\item $(4,8)$ also fails. In general, at $N=2n$ the element $a_n=\pb_{\wo[1,n-1]}\,\pi_n\,\pb_{\wo[n+1,2n-1]}$ gives $a_n\cdot1_\mu\in B$. That half is proved; the ``not in $A$'' half is checked exactly only at $n=3,4$.
\item Open: is $A=B$ at $(4,7)$? That decides between ``$A=B\iff N\le n+2$'' and ``$A=B\iff N\le 2n-1$ ($n\ge3$)''.
\end{itemize}
