## 2. Verdicts on pre-registered predictions

**(a) Divisibility: CONFIRMED** for r = 1..8 (`k4_factor.log`).
- Q_r := q^10 τ^(4) [4]_t / ((q^4−1)[r+4]_t) is a polynomial for every r = 1..8.
- The only Φ_d(t) with d | r+4 that are missing from q^10 τ are those with d | gcd(r+4, 4), d > 1, i.e. d ∈ {2, 4}:
  - r odd: none missing.
  - r ≡ 2 mod 4: Φ_2 missing.
  - r ≡ 0 mod 4: Φ_2 and Φ_4 missing.
- No cyclotomic factors appear beyond those of [r+4]_t. This is the same rule as at k=3.
- Other factors: (q−1)(q+1)(q²+1) = q^4−1, and one irreducible bivariate factor of q-degree 6.

**(b) Cofactor cubic in u: CONFIRMED** (`k4_fit.log`, `k4_closed_form_check.log`).
- Ansatz: Q_r = Σ_{j≤3} p_j(q,t) u^j with deg_q ≤ 6 and deg_t ≤ 8, i.e. 252 unknowns.
- Fitted on r = 5, 6, 7, 8. The solution is unique (0 free parameters).
- Held-out checks:
  - exact zero difference for r = 1, 2, 3, 4 (all m);
  - r = 9 at q = 1/3 with t symbolic (`k4_check_sspec.py`);
  - r = 10: see §4.
- P_3 is irreducible over Q.

**(c) Leading u³ coefficient has t-part t⁶: CONFIRMED.**
- The coefficient is −q^6 t^6.
- Across k = 2, 3, 4, the leading term of τ is (−1)^{k−1} q^{C(k,2)} t^{C(k,2)} u^{k−1} · (q^k−1)[r+k]_t / ([k]_t q^{C(k+1,2)}), with q-normalisation exponents 3, 6, 10. The C(k+1,2) pattern in the q-normalisation is an observation, not a prediction.

## 3. Closed form (`computed`; fit r = 5..8, held out r = 1..4, 9 [q=1/3], 10 [see §4])

    q^10 τ_r^(4) = −(q^4−1) [r+4]_t · P̂_3(t^r, t) / [4]_t

Write N_j := ∏_{i=1}^{j} (t^i u − 1), the Newton basis. Then

    P̂_3 = q^6 N_3 + q^5 [4]_t N_2 + q^3 (1+t²)(q(t³+t²−1) + 1 + t) N_1 + [4]_t (q(t−1)+1)(q²(t²−1)+1).

The same expansion at the lower levels (`k4_newton_basis.log`), with the sign normalised so the leading coefficient is positive:

| k | N_{k−1} | N_{k−2} | N_1 (k=4 only) | N_0 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | q | — | — | [2]_t |
| 3 | q^3 | q^2 [3]_t | — | [3]_t (q(t−1)+1) |
| 4 | q^6 | q^5 [4]_t | q^3 Φ_4(t)(q(t³+t²−1)+1+t) | [4]_t (q(t−1)+1)(q²(t²−1)+1) |

- **Pattern (`hunch`, three data points, and k=2 is degenerate).** N_{k−1}: q^{C(k,2)}. N_{k−2}: q^{C(k,2)−1}[k]_t. N_0: [k]_t ∏_{i=1}^{k−2}(q^i(t^i−1)+1).
- **What breaks the naive form.** The guessed form "q^{C(k,2)} N_{k−1} + [k]_t·(lower)" is REFUTED at k=4. The N_1 coefficient carries only Φ_4, not Φ_2·Φ_4 = [4]_t. At t=1 it becomes 2q^4 + 4q^3, which is not divisible by 4.
- **How the cyclotomic behaviour follows.** P̂_3(i^r, i) = 0 for r ≢ 0 mod 4, and P̂_3((−1)^r, −1) = 0 for r odd. At r ≡ 0 mod 4 the values are −4q^6 and 4q^4 respectively, both nonzero. This explains the table in (a).
