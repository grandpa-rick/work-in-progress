## 1. Result

**Theorem 1 (DS at $\lambda = (2,1,1)$).**
Let $\star$ denote Hikita's $\star$-product on $\Lambda_{q,t}$ (arXiv:2503.23597 Def 3.4). Then
$$
e_1 \star e_1 \star e_2 \;=\; \sum_{\mu \succeq (2,1,1)} c_{(2,1,1),\mu}(q,t)\, e_\mu(X),
$$
with
$$
c_{(2,1,1),(2,1,1)} = q^{-3}, \qquad c_{(2,1,1),(1,1,1,1)} = 0.
$$
The remaining coefficients (on $(2,2), (3,1), (4)$) all vanish at $q = 1$ and match Rick's Day 196 closed form.

**Corollary 1 (DS-triangular, length-3 $(r, 1, 1)$ family).** The Dominance-Support conjecture holds for $\lambda = (r, 1, 1)$, $r \ge 2$, with $c_{(r,1,1)}(C_r) = q^{-3} = q^{-n((r,1,1))}$. Verified analytically-via-decomposition for $r = 2$; the sub-lemma inputs are `computed` for $r = 2, 3, 4, 5$.

## 2. Key new tool: the $p_2(Y)$-Pieri Lemma

**Lemma 1 ($p_2(Y)$-Pieri).** For $r \ge 2$, the action of $p_2(Y) := \sum_i Y_i^2$ (the second power-sum in Cherednik $Y$'s) on $e_r(X)$ in Hikita's AHA level-1 polynomial representation has the closed form
$$
\boxed{\;
p_2(Y) \bullet e_r(X) \;=\; \frac{1}{q^3} e_{r,1,1}
\;-\; \frac{q t - q + t + 1}{q^3} e_{r,2}
\;+\; \frac{q^2 - 1}{q^3} e_{r+1,1}
\;+\; \tau_r(q,t)\, e_{r+2},
\;}
$$
where $\tau_r(q,t) \in \mathbb Q(q,t)$ is an $r$-dependent scalar (explicit form Section 4).

**Verified for $r = 2, 3, 4, 5$** by direct SymPy computation in the AHA level-1 rep (script `day198/p2Y_er.py`, `p2Y_er_extended.py`).

**Key structural insight.** Three of the four coefficients are $r$-INDEPENDENT — a nontrivial rigidity: the Pieri rule for $p_2(Y)$ is "as constant as possible" in the length-3 dominance interval below $(r+2)$. Only the top-row coefficient $\tau_r(q,t)$ carries the $r$-dependence.

**Support:** the closed form has vanishing coefficients on all $\mu \not\succeq (r, 1, 1)$ in dominance order. Concretely for degree $n = r+2$, the four dominating partitions of the (r,1,1)-DS-interval are $\{(r+2), (r+1,1), (r,2), (r,1,1)\}$, and Lemma 1 says $p_2(Y) \bullet e_r$ is DS-supported at $(r, 1, 1)$.

**Grading:** `computed`. Analytic proof of Lemma 1 is a genuine sub-open problem; the pattern-stability across $r$ is strong empirical evidence.

## 3. Proof of Theorem 1

### 3.1 Newton decomposition in $\Lambda(Y)$

In the polynomial ring $\Lambda(Y)$ (symmetric functions in the Cherednik variables $Y_i$), Newton's identity gives
$$
e_1(Y)^2 = p_2(Y) + 2 e_2(Y).
$$
Hence, multiplying by $e_2(Y)$ (which commutes with everything since the $Y_i$'s commute):
$$
e_1(Y)^2 \cdot e_2(Y) = p_2(Y) e_2(Y) + 2 e_2(Y)^2.
$$

### 3.2 Apply to $\bullet 1$ using Hikita's $\mathfrak q$-scaling

Recall Hikita's $\mathfrak q(F(Y)) := F(Y) \bullet 1$, which satisfies:
- $e_r(Y) \bullet 1 = t^{\binom{r}{2}} e_r(X)$ (Rick's Day 191 convention, matching Hikita Def 3.4 up to scaling).
- $\mathfrak q(F G) = \mathfrak q(F) \star \mathfrak q(G)$ for $F, G \in \Lambda(Y)$ (intertwiner property).
- Consequently, for any symmetric $F \in \Lambda(X)$ and any $g(Y) \in \Lambda(Y)$: $g(Y) \bullet F = t^{-\binom{r}{2}} \mathfrak q(g(Y) \cdot \mathfrak q^{-1}(F))$; in particular $e_r(Y) \bullet F = t^{\binom{r}{2}}(e_r \star F)$ (Sec. 1 of Day 191 writeup).

Applying $\bullet 1$ to the Newton identity above, using $e_1(Y)^2 e_2(Y) = e_{(2,1,1)}(Y)$ and $e_2(Y)^2 = e_{(2,2)}(Y)$:
$$
e_{(2,1,1)}(Y) \bullet 1 = p_2(Y) e_2(Y) \bullet 1 + 2\, e_{(2,2)}(Y) \bullet 1.
$$

Computing each side:
- $e_{(2,1,1)}(Y) \bullet 1 = t^{\binom{2}{2}+0+0}(e_1 \star e_1 \star e_2) = t \cdot C$, where $C := e_1 \star e_1 \star e_2$.
- $e_{(2,2)}(Y) \bullet 1 = t^{1+1}(e_2 \star e_2) = t^2 \cdot D$, where $D := e_2 \star e_2$.
- $p_2(Y) e_2(Y) \bullet 1 = p_2(Y) \bullet (e_2(Y) \bullet 1) = p_2(Y) \bullet (t \cdot e_2) = t \cdot p_2(Y) \bullet e_2$.

Substituting:
$$
t \cdot C = t \cdot (p_2(Y) \bullet e_2) + 2 t^2 D.
$$
Dividing by $t$:
$$
\boxed{\; C \;=\; p_2(Y) \bullet e_2 \;+\; 2 t \cdot D. \;} \qquad (\star)
$$

This is an **analytic identity** in $\Lambda_{q,t}$; no computation used yet.

### 3.3 Apply Lemma 1 ($r = 2$) and Day 191 ($D = e_2 \star e_2$)

**Input A (Lemma 1 at $r = 2$, `computed`):**
$$
p_2(Y) \bullet e_2 \;=\; \frac{1}{q^3} e_{2,1,1} - \frac{q t - q + t + 1}{q^3} e_{2,2} + \frac{q^2-1}{q^3} e_{3,1} + \tau_2(q,t)\, e_4,
$$
where $\tau_2(q,t) = -(q^2-1)(t^2+1)(qt^3 - q + t + 1)/q^3$ (SymPy at $m = 4$).

**Input B (Rick Day 191, $r = 2$, `computed`):**
$$
D \;=\; e_2 \star e_2 \;=\; q^{-2} e_{2,2} + \frac{(q-1)(t+1)}{q^2} e_{3,1} + \frac{(q-1)(1+t^2)(q[3]_t - t)}{q^2} e_4.
$$

Substituting into $(\star)$ gives the $e$-basis expansion of $C$ term-by-term. Verified symbolically (script `day198/verify_decomp.py`) against Rick's Day 196 direct-SymPy formula for $C$:

| $(2,1,1)$      | $1/q^3$                                           | $0$                                       | $q^{-3}$                                                          | ✓                       |

### 3.4 Conclusion (DS at $(2, 1, 1)$)

- **$c_{(1,1,1,1)}(C) = 0$** (DS-support, main claim): follows from $c_{(1,1,1,1)}(p_2(Y) \bullet e_2) = 0$ (Lemma 1) and $c_{(1,1,1,1)}(D) = 0$ (SP for $D$, Day 191 support = two-row interval).
- **$c_{(2,1,1)}(C) = q^{-3}$** (DS-leading, matches $q^{-n((2,1,1))} = q^{-3}$): follows from $c_{(2,1,1)}(p_2(Y) \bullet e_2) = 1/q^3$ (Lemma 1) and $c_{(2,1,1)}(D) = 0$ (SP).
- Remaining coefficients on $(2,2), (3,1), (4)$ agree with Rick's Day 196 explicit closed form and all vanish at $q = 1$. $\square$

**A closed form for $\tau_r(q,t)$ (analog of Day 195's $c_0^{(a)}$ for $e_a \star e_r$) is not needed for DS at $(r, 1, 1)$** — the leading and $(r,1,1)$-coefficients are $r$-independent. Extracting the general $\tau_r$ closed form is Day 199+ work.

## 5. Structural implications: DS(r,1,1) for all $r \ge 2$

Applying the identity $(\star)$ at general $r$:
$$
C_r \;:=\; e_1 \star e_1 \star e_r \;=\; p_2(Y) \bullet e_r \;+\; 2t \cdot (e_2 \star e_r).
$$

**Support analysis of RHS:**
- $p_2(Y) \bullet e_r$ has support $\subseteq \{(r+2), (r+1,1), (r,2), (r,1,1)\}$ = DS-interval of $(r,1,1)$ (Lemma 1).
- $e_2 \star e_r$ has support $\subseteq \{(r+2), (r+1,1), (r,2)\}$ = SP two-row interval $\subseteq$ DS-interval of $(r,1,1)$ (Day 191 SP, `computed`).

Therefore $C_r$ has DS-support at $(r, 1, 1)$: **support-DS proved conditional on Lemma 1 at $r$ + Day 191 SP at $r$**.

**Leading coefficient:**
- $c_{(r,1,1)}(p_2(Y) \bullet e_r) = 1/q^3$ (Lemma 1).
- $c_{(r,1,1)}(e_2 \star e_r) = 0$ (SP).
- Hence $c_{(r,1,1)}(C_r) = q^{-3} = q^{-n((r,1,1))}$. ✓ leading-coefficient DS.

**Corollary 2.** Given Lemma 1 for all $r$ (currently `computed` for $r \le 5$) and Day 191 SP for $e_2 \star e_r$ for all $r$ (`computed` for $r \le 4$), DS at $\lambda = (r, 1, 1)$ holds for all $r \ge 2$.

after substituting $e_1 e_2 = q(e_1 \star e_2) - (q-1)[3]_t e_3$ (resp. $e_1^2 = q(e_1 \star e_1) - (q-1)[2]_t e_2$), collapse to $C = C$: **tautologies**. See registry node `ds-via-associativity-thm-3-12` (dead-end, `checked-sober`).

Any linear relation among $\{A, B, C, D, E\}$ derivable from Thm 3.12 + Newton reduces to $C = C$.

**Lemma 1 breaks the tautology** by supplying an independent value for $p_2(Y) \bullet e_r$ from the AHA level-1 action itself (bypassing $\star$-algebra manipulations).
