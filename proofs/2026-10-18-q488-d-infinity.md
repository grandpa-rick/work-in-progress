Remark (the PROVE.md wording). PROVE.md said "every jump-≥2 triple for which **Theorem 6** allows an H_ab pole".
Theorem 6 is an aggregate over ν. Its condition (∃M'' ⊆ [N]∖ab with M'' ≥ λ,μ) is strictly weaker than
E(ν) ≠ ∅, and R∞ = 0 for the triples in between. At N=4 there are 354 such (triple, pair) cases on the five pairs
≠ (1,2), and all of them have R0 = R∞ = 0. The correct per-ν condition is E ≠ ∅, and with it the equivalence is
exact (census, §8).

The mechanism, in one paragraph. Motegi's biorthogonality says that the inverse of the q-matrix is the
q⁻¹-matrix at complements. Taking q→∞ gives an **exact dual basis for φ^∞ built from φ⁰ at complements**
(Prop 2). So the q=∞ residue is an explicit finite sum. In the gauge where φ^∞ becomes the N-free family F, the
sum carries a weight Π_{m∈M''} w_m^{c} with **c = −2N** (Prop 1). Nonvanishing for c ≤ −2 then follows by peeling
the top variable. Either a forced head factorises the sum exactly, or the weight w_n^{c} makes the n-containing
terms dominate as w_n → 0. In the leftover "stuck" case, w_n → ∞ shifts ν to ν̃ without changing E. The base
case is the number 1. At q=0 the same sum has weight c = 0, and the dominance step ties at exactly the
separator triple (§7).

## 1. The q=∞ basis in closed form

Notation. T⁰_κ(h) is the summand of (2.1): for κ = (κ_1<…<κ_r), M ⊆ [N] and an injective "head map" h: [r]→M
(h_j carries label κ_j), let x_j := w_{h_j} and let tails be t ∈ M∖h. Then
T⁰_κ(h) = Π_j (1−x_j⁻²) Π_{k<κ_j}(1−w_k/x_j) · Π_{j<k} (1−1/(x_jx_k))/(1−x_j/x_k) · Π_{t,j} (1−1/(w_t x_j))/(1−w_t/x_j),
and φ⁰_κ(M) = Σ_h T⁰_κ(h) (Day 243 (2.1); terms with h_j < κ_j vanish identically).

**Definition.** F_κ(M) := Π_{m∈M} w_m^{2r} · Σ_h T⁰_κ(h) · Π_j x_j^{κ_j − 2j}   (r = |κ|).

F is N-free, and F_κ(M) = 0 unless M ≥ κ (by Prop 1 and (M3)). For M = κ only h = id
survives (any other injective h has some h_j < κ_j), so **F_κ(κ) = φ⁰_κ(κ)·Π_{j} w_{κ_j}^{κ_j−2j+2r}**.

**Proposition 1 (closed form).** For all M, I ⊆ [N]:
  φ^∞_I(M) := lim_{q→∞} q^{−(dim − |I|)} Ã_{M,I} = τ(I) · Π_{m∈M} w_m^{−2N} · F_I(M),
where |I| = Σ_{i∈I} i, and τ(I) ≠ 0 is a Laurent monomial in w of the multiplicative form
τ(I) = τ_∅ Π_{i∈I} χ(i). (Computed: τ(I) = const/c(I), c(I) := Π_{i∈I} (−1)^i Π_{k≤i} w_k.)

*Proof.* Use (M1) unpadded, n = s = |M| (M5), with u = w_M. For a fixed ordering (heads u_1..u_r with labels
I, tails u_{r+1..s}), each factor of Motegi's summand is a polynomial in q. Its q-leading coefficient is:
- head: (1−q)(u²−1)Π_{k>i}(u−qw_k)Π_{k<i}(u−w_k) → (−1)^{1+N−i}(u²−1)Π_{k>i}w_kΠ_{k<i}(u−w_k);
- head pair j<k: (qu_j−u_k) → u_j;
- tail: Π_k(u−qw_k)·u → (−1)^N W_N u, with W_N := Π_{k≤N} w_k;
- tail–head: (qu_t − u_j) → u_t;
- tail pair: (u_tu_{t'} − q) → −1.
All orderings have the same q-degree d = Σ_j(1+N−i_j) + C(r,2) + N(s−r) + r(s−r) + C(s−r,2). Divide each
leading term by the corresponding q=0 term of the same ordering (Day 243 §2bis lists those). The ratio is
± W_N^{s−r} Π_jΠ_{k>i_j} w_k times the u-monomial
Π_j u_j^{i_j − 2j + 2r + 1 − N − s} Π_t u_t^{2r+1−N−s}
= Π_{m∈M} m^{2r+1−N−s} · Π_j u_j^{i_j−2j}.
This is invariant under tail permutations. So the q=0 bookkeeping (each ordering collects u^{N+s}, and the
(s−r)! tail orderings cancel) goes through verbatim and gives
LC_q(F) = ± W_N^{s−r} Π_jΠ_{k>i_j}w_k · Π_{m∈M} m^{2r+1−N−s} · Σ_h T⁰_I(h)Π_j x_j^{i_j−2j}.
E^C (M2, unpadded) is a product of factors (1 − q·monomial)^{±1} and (1−q)^{−(s−r)}. Its leading term is
± Π_{m∈M} m^{N−s+1} W_N^{s}/W_N^{N+1}, of degree −(s−r) + s² + sN − C(s,2) − dim. Hence deg_q Ã_{M,I} ≤ d − deg E
= dim − |I|. (The count uses C(r,2) + C(r+1,2) = r² and C(s−r,2) + C(s,2) = s² − rs − s + C(r+1,2).) The
q^{dim−|I|} coefficient is LC(F)/LC(E). In it the W_N^{s} cancels, leaving W_N^{N+1−r}Π_jΠ_{k>i_j}w_k, a function
of I alone, and Π m^{2r+1−N−s}/Π m^{N−s+1} = Π_{m∈M} m^{2r−2N}. The total sign exponent collapses mod 2 to
(I-dependent) + 2Ns + 2s², so the sign depends on I only. Finally Π_{k>i}w_k = W_N/Π_{k≤i}w_k gives the
multiplicative form of τ. ∎

Checked: for N=2,3,4 (from the stored full-q Ã, at a random rational point), lead_∞(Ã_{M,I}) / (Π_{m∈M}w_m^{−2N} F_I(M)/c(I)) takes a
single constant value over all 2^N × 2^N entries (`gauge.py`).

## 2. The exact dual basis

**Motegi's theorem** (2610.15344, L4365–4411, all-cardinality biorthogonality, type C). Let A be Ã with each
row M divided by its diagonal Δ_M := Ã_{M,M}. Then (A(q)⁻¹)_{I,L} = q^{|I|−|L|} A_{L^c,I^c}(q⁻¹)
(complements in [N]; |·| = sum of parts).

**Proposition 2 (dual of φ^∞).** For all ν, L ⊆ [N], with ℓ^∞ the coordinates in the basis φ^∞:
  ℓ^∞_ν(e_L) = φ⁰_{ν^c}(L^c) / ( φ^∞_L(L) · φ⁰_{L^c}(L^c) ).
Equivalently, in the basis F:
  **ℓ^F_ν(e_L) = [τ(ν)/τ(L)] · φ⁰_{ν^c}(L^c) / ( F_L(L) · φ⁰_{L^c}(L^c) ).**   (D)

*Proof.* (Ã⁻¹)_{νL} = (A⁻¹)_{νL}/Δ_L(q) = q^{|ν|−|L|} Ã_{L^c,ν^c}(q⁻¹) / (Δ_{L^c}(q⁻¹) Δ_L(q)). By Prop 1,
Ã(q) = Φ^∞ (1 + O(1/q)) diag(q^{dim−|I|}), and Φ^∞ is triangular with nonzero diagonal (F_L(L) is a monomial
times φ⁰_L(L) ≠ 0). So (Φ^∞)⁻¹_{νL} = lim_q q^{dim−|ν|}(Ã⁻¹)_{νL}
= lim q^{dim−|L|} Ã_{L^c,ν^c}(q⁻¹)/(Δ_{L^c}(q⁻¹)Δ_L(q)). As q→∞, Ã_{L^c,ν^c}(q⁻¹) → φ⁰_{ν^c}(L^c),
Δ_{L^c}(q⁻¹) → φ⁰_{L^c}(L^c), and q^{−(dim−|L|)}Δ_L(q) → φ^∞_L(L). For (D), substitute φ^∞ = τ·D_N·F with
D_N(M) := Π_{m∈M}w_m^{−2N}: ℓ^F_ν(f) = τ(ν) ℓ^∞_ν(D_N f). ∎

Checked (exact rational arithmetic, random points): Prop 2 at N=2,3 (`biorth.py`, 16/16 and 64/64, plus the
mirror statement for ℓ⁰); (D) with τ(ν)/τ(L) = c(L)/c(ν) at N=4 (256/256, two points) and N=5 (1024/1024)
(`fdual_num.py`).

Two consequences used below.
- (D) does not depend on the ambient N. If n ∉ ν, L, then n ∈ ν^c and n ∈ L^c. The label n forces the head n
  in both φ⁰ factors (Day 243 Prop 8 with one forced head), and the forced factor e⁰_n(L^c) cancels between
  numerator and denominator. Of course this also follows from ℓ_ν seeing only ↓ν.
- Residue functional on H = H_ab. For L ⊇ ab: L^c ∌ a,b, so φ⁰_{ν^c}(L^c) and φ⁰_{L^c}(L^c) are regular on H,
  and the latter is ≠ 0 there. Its diagonal factors are the S, L, A factors of L^c, and the L-factor
  (1−1/(w_aw_b)) is absent. F_L(L) has exactly one factor h := 1−1/(w_aw_b) (M4). Hence
  **ρ_{νL} := Res_H ℓ^F_ν(e_L) = [τ(ν)/τ(L)]·φ⁰_{ν^c}(L^c) / (φ⁰_{L^c}(L^c)·(F_L(L)/h)) |_H**, a closed form.

## 3. ∞-Theorem 7 (P2a), proved

*Proof (Day 243 §2, §4, §5 transported to p = 1/q).* Put φ̂_I := q^{−(dim−|I|)}Ã_I. Then φ̂_λφ̂_μ = Σ_ν ĉ^ν φ̂_ν

q⁻¹(1 − q m) = −m(1 − p m⁻¹). So φ̂ ∈ R' := C[p, w^±] localised at the units (1 − p·monomial), and
φ̂|_{p=0} = φ^∞. R' ⊂ C[p,w^±]_{(p)}, a DVR with residue field C(w). det φ̂ is the product of diagonals, and the
diagonals are units because φ^∞_I(I) ≠ 0. So ĉ ∈ C(w)[[p]], and differentiating gives (1.1) with

Lemma QC is a statement about Ã with the same normalisation on both sides, so it holds for φ̂. In p,

## 4. Reduction to a weighted problem

By Prop 1, P^∞ := φ^∞_λφ^∞_μ = τ(λ)τ(μ)D_N² F_λF_μ and ℓ^∞_ν(f) = ℓ^F_ν(f/D_N)/τ(ν). On H we have
D_N(M) = D_N(M'')(w_aw_b)^{−2N}, so D_N(M) ≡ D_N(M'') mod h. Since the residue only sees defects mod h:

  Res ℓ^∞_ν(1_{⊇ab}P^∞) = [τ(λ)τ(μ)/τ(ν)] · R^A(λ,μ,ν; a,b; c)  with **c = −2N**, where

**(A)** R^A(λ,μ,ν;a,b;c) := Res_{H_ab} ℓ^F_ν(1_{⊇ab} · w^c · F_λF_μ) = Σ_{L ⊇ ab} ρ_{νL} · w_{L''}^c F_λF_μ(L'')|_H,
with w_S^c := Π_{s∈S} w_s^c and L'' := L∖{a,b}.
By triangularity, only L ≤ ν (ρ) and L'' ≥ λ, μ (F) contribute. So **R^A is a sum over E(λ,μ,ν;ab)**.

Two auxiliary problems appear in the induction (system [n], x := w_a a free variable):
**(B)** B(λ,μ,κ;a;c) := ℓ^F_κ(G), with G(M) := [a∈M] · w_S^c F_λF_μ(S)/g_a(S), S := M∖a,
g_a(S) := Π_{s∈S} w_s²(1−x/w_s)/(1−xw_s). Its support is E_B := {S ⊆ [n]∖a : S∪a ≤ κ, S ≥ λ, μ}.
**(C)** C(λ,μ,κ;c) := ℓ^F_κ(w^c F_λF_μ). Its support is E_C := {M ⊆ [n] : M ≤ κ, M ≥ λ, μ}.
All three are explicit finite sums by (D).

## 5. Local lemmas (u := w_n, n = top of the system [n])

**L1 (forced head, exact).** If n ∈ κ and n ∈ M, then label n must sit on head n. With
e⁰_n(M) := (1−u⁻²) Π_{c∈M⁻}(1−1/(cu)) Π_{k<n, k∉M⁻}(1−w_k/u) and M⁻ := M∖n, κ⁻ := κ∖n:
  φ⁰_κ(M) = e⁰_n(M) φ⁰_{κ⁻}(M⁻),   F_κ(M) = e⁰_n(M) u^n Π_{c∈M⁻}w_c² · F_{κ⁻}(M⁻).
*Proof.* This is Day 243 Prop 8 with a single forced head. The cross factor of n with every other element of
M (head or tail) is label-free, and its denominator cancels against the factor k=c of Π_{k<n}(1−w_k/u). For F,
the extra weight is u^{n−2r}, and the prefactors differ by u^{2r} Π_{M⁻} w² . ∎
e⁰_n(M) is a Laurent polynomial in u. Its lowest term is (−1)^n W u^{−(n+1)} Π_{c∈M⁻} w_c^{−2}, with
W := Π_{k<n} w_k. Its limit as u→∞ is 1. Hence
F_κ(M)/F_{κ⁻}(M⁻) = (−1)^n W u⁻¹ (1+O(u)), the same leading term for every M.

**L2 (u→0, head n not forced).** Let n ∈ M and κ ⊆ [n−1], κ ≠ ∅. Then F_κ(M) = u⁻¹(K_κ F_{κ⁻}(M⁻) + O(u)),
where κ⁻ := κ minus its largest element and K_κ := (−1)^{κ_r} Π_{k<κ_r} w_k. Also F_∅ ≡ 1.
*Proof.* Termwise u-orders: n as head j has order r−j−1, and n as a tail has order r. Only "n is the last head"
reaches order −1. In those terms the factors involving n tend to −u⁻², (−1)^{κ_r−1}u^{1−κ_r}Π_{k<κ_r}w_k,
w_h^{−2} (earlier heads), w_t^{−2} (tails), and u^{κ_r} (weight and prefactor). The w^{−2}'s turn the prefactor
Π_M w^{2r} into Π_{M⁻} w^{2(r−1)}. If κ_r = n, L1 gives the same leading term. ∎

**L3 (u→∞).** Let n ∈ M, κ ⊆ [n−1], κ ≠ ∅. Then φ⁰_κ(M) → φ⁰_{κ⁻}(M⁻), with κ⁻ := κ minus its largest element.
*Proof.* Termwise degree: n as head j has degree −(r−j), and n as a tail has degree −r. The degree-0 terms are
exactly "n is the last head", and their factors involving n all tend to 1. ∎

**L4 (out-term bound).** Let n ∈ L^c and κ ⊆ [n−1] (n ∈ ν, κ = ν^c). Then
ord_{u=0} φ⁰_κ(L^c)/φ⁰_{L^c}(L^c) ≥ 1.
*Proof.* Termwise, ord_0 φ⁰_κ(L^c) ≥ min(−r', −1 − r' − max_j(κ_j − j)) ≥ −n, since κ_j − j ≤ (n−1) − r'.
Also φ⁰_{L^c}(L^c) = e⁰_n(L^c)φ⁰_{L^c⁻}(L^c⁻) by L1, and 1/e⁰_n has order exactly n+1. ∎

**L5 (shift).** Let n ∈ ν ≠ [n], let m* := max([n]∖ν), and put ν̃ := (ν∖n) ∪ {m*} ⊆ [n−1]. If n ∉ L and L ≤ ν,
then L ≤ ν̃. Also ν̃ ≤ ν, and the complement of ν̃ in [n−1] is ν^c∖{m*}.
*Proof.* ν ⊇ (m*, n]. Top-aligned, ν̃ replaces the top block (m*+1, …, n) by (m*, …, n−1). Any L ∌ n has
i-th-from-top element ≤ n−i, and below the block ν̃ = ν. ∎

**L6 (replacement).** If S ∈ E and the largest element of S∪{a,b} lies in S (for (B): of S∪{a}), then replacing
that element by n gives an element of E containing n. This holds because raising the top element preserves
both ≤ ν (top of ν is n) and ≥ λ, μ. Consequently:
- in (A), if no element of E contains n, then every S ∈ E lies in [b−1];
- in (C), it is impossible unless E_C = {∅}, which forces λ = μ = ∅; but then {n} ∈ E_C, so it never happens.

## 6. Theorem W (weighted nonvanishing)

**Theorem W.** Let n ≥ 0 and c ≤ −2 be an integer.
(A) For a<b ≤ n: R^A(λ,μ,ν;a,b;c) ≠ 0 ⟺ E ≠ ∅.
(B) For a ≤ n: B(λ,μ,κ;a;c) ≠ 0 ⟺ E_B ≠ ∅.
(C) C(λ,μ,κ;c) ≠ 0 ⟺ E_C ≠ ∅.
(⇒ is triangularity. We prove ⇐.)

*Proof.* Induction on n, case by case on the top variable u = w_n. Each case either reduces exactly, or
compares u-orders and identifies the leading coefficient as a nonzero constant times a smaller instance. Every
step keeps the support nonempty and keeps c ≤ −2 (c only decreases).

(C) base: n = 0 gives C(∅,∅,∅) = 1/F_∅(∅) = 1.
- **C1** (n ∉ κ): (D) is ambient-free, so C_{[n]} = C_{[n−1]}. E_C is unchanged, since M ≤ κ forces n ∉ M; if
  n ∈ λ, then E_C = ∅.
- **C2** (n ∈ κ): some M ∈ E_C contains n (L6). In-terms (M ∋ n): by (D) and L1, τ(κ)/τ(M) and the dual
  ratio are u-free and equal to their [n−1] versions for (κ⁻, M⁻). Also 1/F_M(M) = u·(const + O(u))/F_{M⁻}(M⁻),
  w_M^c = u^c w_{M⁻}^c, and F_λF_μ(M) = u^{−δ}(K_λK_μ F_{λ⁻}F_{μ⁻}(M⁻) + O(u)) (L2), with
  δ := [λ≠∅] + [μ≠∅]. Their sum is u^{c+1−δ}(K·C_{[n−1]}(λ⁻,μ⁻,κ⁻;c) + O(u)), with K ≠ 0.
  Out-terms (M ∌ n): τ(κ)/τ(M) ∝ u⁻¹, the dual ratio has order ≥ 1 (L4), and the rest is u-free. So they are O(1).
  Since c+1−δ ≤ −1 < 0 and E_C(λ⁻,μ⁻,κ⁻) is the image of E_C ∩ {∋n} under M ↦ M⁻ (nonempty), induction gives
  C_{[n]} ≠ 0.
(B) (a ≤ n):
- **B1** (n ∉ κ, n ≠ a): exact, as in C1.
- **B2** (n ∈ κ, n ≠ a, some S ∈ E_B contains n): as in C2, with the extra factor
  1/g_a = (1−xu)/(u(u−x)) = −(xu)⁻¹(1+O(u)). In-terms are u^{c−δ}(K·B_{[n−1]}(λ⁻,μ⁻,κ⁻;a;c) + O(u)), out-terms
  are O(1), and c−δ < 0.
- **B3** (n ∈ κ, n ≠ a, stuck): every term has n ∈ M^c. As u→∞: τ(κ)/τ(M) = u⁻¹·const·τ(κ̃)/τ(M) (τ is
  multiplicative). The dual ratio tends to its [n−1] version for κ̃ (L3 and e⁰_n → 1). Everything else is
  u-free. So u·B_{[n]}(κ) → const·B_{[n−1]}(κ̃), and E_B(κ̃) = E_B(κ) by L5. (κ ≠ [n], because for κ = [n] the
  set S∪{n} is in E_B.)
- **B4** (n = a; necessarily a ∈ κ, else E_B = ∅): every M contains n. The dual ratio and τ-ratio are exactly the
  [n−1] ones for (κ⁻, S). By L1, F_M(M) = F_S(S)·e⁰_n(S∪n)x^n Π_{s∈S}w_s². Per s ∈ S, the factors are
  w_s²(1−x/w_s)/(1−xw_s) · (1−1/(w_sx))/(1−w_s/x) · w_s² = w_s², using (1−x/s)/(1−s/x) = −x/s and
  (1−1/(sx))/(1−xs) = −1/(xs). Hence **B_{[n]}(λ,μ,κ;n;c) = K·C_{[n−1]}(λ,μ,κ⁻;c−2)** exactly, with
  E_C ↔ E_B (S∪n ≤ κ ⟺ S ≤ κ⁻).
(A) (a<b ≤ n):
- **A1** (n ∉ ν, n ≠ b): exact (n > b; L ≤ ν forces n ∉ L).
- **A2** (n ∈ ν, n ≠ b, some element of E contains n): as in C2, on H. In-terms are
  u^{c+1−δ}(K·R^A_{[n−1]}(λ⁻,μ⁻,ν⁻) + O(u)). This uses L1 for F_L(L), where a,b ∈ L⁻ keeps the factor h
  inside F_{L⁻}(L⁻). Out-terms are O(1) by L4 and τ(ν)/τ(L) ∝ u⁻¹. c+1−δ < 0.
- **A3** (n ∈ ν, n ≠ b, stuck): as in B3, u·R^A_{[n]}(ν) → const·R^A_{[n−1]}(ν̃) with E(ν̃) = E(ν) (L5).
- **A4** (n = b; then n ∈ ν, else E = ∅): every L = L''∪{a,n}. The dual ratio and τ-ratio are the [n−1] ones
  for (ν⁻, L⁻). On H (w_n = 1/x):
  F_L(L)/h = F_{L⁻}(L⁻)·(1−x²)x^{2−n} Π_{k<n,k≠a}(1−w_kx) · g_a(L''),
  because e⁰_n(L)/h = (1−x²) Π_{s∈L''}(1−x/w_s) Π_{k∉L⁻}(1−w_kx). Hence **R^A_{[n]}(λ,μ,ν;a,n;c) =
