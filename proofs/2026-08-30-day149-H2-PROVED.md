## 0. What was open at the start of the session

Day 148 proved the target theorem $3\mid b_k$ outright. Two riders were left:

* **(H2)** $\deg_{E_3}[T^n]H\le\lfloor n/3\rfloor$, $H:=\tau(F_P)/F_P$ — still an *assumption*.
  It is the hypothesis of Day 146 Theorem 2, hence of $\mathcal H=\frac{F(F-1)}{\vartheta(2F-3)}$,
  hence of Day 148's Corollary $\mathcal H\in\mathbb Z[[\vartheta]]$.
* **(H1)** $H\in\mathbb Z[E_1,E_2,E_3][[T]]$. Untouched.

Both proofs of (H2) below have the same shape and it is worth stating it up front, because it
is the reusable idea:

> **Take a filtration in which $\tau$ cannot move the extreme layer. Then $\log H=(\tau-1)\log F_P$
> loses that layer for free, and $H=\exp(\log H)$ inherits the improved bound.**

Proof A uses the $E_3$-order filtration, where "$\tau$ cannot move the bottom layer" needs
Day 148's Cor 5.2 ($E_1,E_2$-freeness). Proof B uses the $u$-degree filtration, where the
same statement is trivial ($\tau(E_i)=E_i+\text{lower}$). Proof B is therefore both cleaner
and stronger, and I give it as the official proof.

## 1. Notation

$u_1,u_2,u_3$ indeterminates, $E_i=e_i(u)$, $V=\prod_{i<j}(u_i-u_j)$,
$\mathcal T:u^\alpha\mapsto\prod_i(u_i)_{\alpha_i}$ (falling factorials),
$\mathcal T^+:u^\alpha\mapsto\prod_iu_i^{(\alpha_i)}$ (rising), $\Psi(f)=\mathcal T(fV)/V$,
$\Psi_b=\Psi(e_2^b)$, $\varphi:u_i\mapsto-u_i$, $P_b=\varphi(\Psi_b)\in\mathbb Z[E]$,
$$F_P=\sum_{b\ge0}P_b\frac{T^b}{b!}=\frac{\mathcal T^+\!\left(e^{Te_2}V\right)}{V}\ \in\ \mathbb Q[E_1,E_2,E_3][[T]],\qquad F_P=1+O(T).$$
$\tau$ = the automorphism of $\mathbb Q[E]$ induced by $u_i\mapsto u_i+1$:
$E_1\mapsto E_1+3$, $E_2\mapsto E_2+2E_1+3$, $E_3\mapsto E_3+E_2+E_1+1$; extended to
$\mathbb Q[E][[T]]$ with $\tau T=T$. $H:=\tau(F_P)/F_P$.

**Two gradings on $\mathbb Q[E][[T]]$.** For a monomial $E_1^iE_2^jE_3^kT^n$:
$$\operatorname{ord}:=n-3k \qquad\text{(the Day 146 order)},\qquad
\operatorname{wt}:=(i+2j+3k)-n \qquad\text{(new: $u$-degree minus $T$-degree)} .$$
Both are additive on products. $\operatorname{ord}X\ge0$ **is** (H2). $\operatorname{wt}X\le0$
says $\deg_u[T^n]X\le n$, and since $\deg_uE_3^k=3k$ it **implies** (H2). Write
$\ell_d$ for the $\operatorname{ord}$-$d$ layer and $\ell^{\mathrm{top}}_w$ for the
$\operatorname{wt}$-$w$ layer.

Elementary and used throughout: within a fixed $T$-degree $n$, $\deg_u[T^n]F_P\le2n$ (from
Lemma 2.1 of Day 148: $\mathcal T^+(u^mV)=\prod u_i^{(m_i)}V(u+m)$ has degree $|m|+3=2n+3$),
so $\operatorname{wt}$ is **bounded above** on each $T$-degree — this is what makes induction
in the $\operatorname{wt}$ grading legitimate, exactly as boundedness below did in Day 148 §4.

## 2. Proof B (official): the $u$-degree bound

### Theorem 1. $\deg_u\bigl([T^n]\log F_P\bigr)\le n+1$, i.e. $\operatorname{wt}(\log F_P)\le1$.

*Proof.* Work in the Horn coordinates of Day 148 §2: with
$\mathcal M(t_1,t_2,t_3)=\sum_{a,b,c}\frac{t_1^at_2^bt_3^c}{a!b!c!}u_1^{(a+b)}u_2^{(a+c)}u_3^{(b+c)}$,
$M_1=u_1+\theta_1+\theta_2$, $M_2=u_2+\theta_1+\theta_3$, $M_3=u_3+\theta_2+\theta_3$, one has
$F_P=V(M)\mathcal M/V(u)\big|_{t=T}$ and the Riccati system (R): with $S=\log\mathcal M$,
$\lambda_i=\theta_iS$, $d_1=\lambda_1+\lambda_2$, $d_2=\lambda_1+\lambda_3$, $d_3=\lambda_2+\lambda_3$,
$D_1=\theta_1+\theta_2$, $D_2=\theta_1+\theta_3$,
$$\lambda_1=t_1\bigl[(u_1{+}d_1)(u_2{+}d_2)+D_1d_2\bigr],\quad
\lambda_2=t_2\bigl[(u_1{+}d_1)(u_3{+}d_3)+D_1d_3\bigr],\quad
\lambda_3=t_3\bigl[(u_2{+}d_2)(u_3{+}d_3)+D_2d_3\bigr].$$

Grade by $\operatorname{wt}(u_i)=1$, $\operatorname{wt}(t_i)=-1$. Then $\theta_i$ preserves
$\operatorname{wt}$, and multiplication by $t_i$ lowers it by $1$.

*Claim: $\operatorname{wt}\bigl([\deg n]\lambda_i\bigr)\le1$ for all $t$-degrees $n$.*
Induction on $n$. For $n=0$: $S$ has $t$-degree $\ge1$, so $\lambda_i$ has no degree-$0$ part.
For $n\ge1$,
$$[\deg n]\lambda_1=t_1\,[\deg (n{-}1)]\bigl\{(u_1{+}d_1)(u_2{+}d_2)+D_1d_2\bigr\}.$$
$D_1d_2$ at degree $n-1$ has $\operatorname{wt}\le1$ by the induction hypothesis ($\theta$'s
preserve $\operatorname{wt}$). In $(u_1{+}d_1)(u_2{+}d_2)$, expanding by $t$-degree, each
factor is taken at a degree $m\le n-1$: at $m=0$ it is $u_i$ ($\operatorname{wt}=1$), at
$m\ge1$ it is $[\deg m]d_j$ ($\operatorname{wt}\le1$ by induction). So the product has
$\operatorname{wt}\le2$, the bracket has $\operatorname{wt}\le2$, and multiplying by $t_1$
gives $\operatorname{wt}\le1$. Same for $\lambda_2,\lambda_3$. $\square$(claim)

Since $\theta_i$ acts on the degree-$\alpha$ component of $S$ by the scalar $\alpha_i$ and
$S$ has no degree-$0$ component, $\operatorname{wt}(S)\le1$ too.

*Prefactor.* $F_P/\mathcal M=\mathcal R/V(u)$ with $\mathcal R:=e^{-S}V(M)e^{S}$. Applying
$M_i-M_j=\delta_{ij}+\Delta_{ij}$ ($\Delta_{ij}$ a difference of $\theta$'s) to $e^S$ gives
$(\delta_{ij}+\Delta_{ij}S)e^S$; iterating, every term of $\mathcal R$ is a product of **at
most three** factors, each of which is either some $\delta_{ij}$ ($\operatorname{wt}=1$) or an
iterated $\Delta$-derivative of $S$ ($\operatorname{wt}\le1$). Hence
$\operatorname{wt}\mathcal R\le3$. As $V(u)$ is $u$-homogeneous of degree $3$ with no $T$,
$\operatorname{wt}(V(u))=3$, so $\operatorname{wt}(\mathcal R/V(u))\le0$. Also
$\mathcal R/V(u)=F_P/\mathcal M\in1+T\,\mathbb Q[u][[T]]$, so its logarithm is a $T$-adically
convergent sum of products of $\operatorname{wt}\le0$ elements, hence has
$\operatorname{wt}\le0$.

Finally $\log F_P=S|_{t=T}+\log(\mathcal R/V(u))|_{t=T}$; restriction to the diagonal
preserves $\operatorname{wt}$ (each $t_i\mapsto T$, both of weight $-1$). So
$\operatorname{wt}(\log F_P)\le\max(1,0)=1$. $\square$

### Theorem 2. $\deg_u\bigl([T^n]H\bigr)\le n$, i.e. $\operatorname{wt}(H)\le0$. In particular **(H2)** holds:
$$\deg_{E_3}\bigl([T^n]H\bigr)\le\lfloor n/3\rfloor .$$

*Proof.* $\log H=\tau(\log F_P)-\log F_P$. For any $p\in\mathbb Q[E]$, $\tau(E_i)=E_i+(\text{terms of
strictly smaller $u$-degree})$, so $\tau p$ has the same $u$-degree as $p$ **and the same
top-degree homogeneous part**. Hence $(\tau-1)$ strictly lowers $u$-degree, i.e.
$$\operatorname{wt}\bigl((\tau-1)X\bigr)\le\operatorname{wt}(X)-1 .$$
With Theorem 1, $\operatorname{wt}(\log H)\le0$. Since $H=1+O(T)$, $\log H\in T\mathbb Q[E][[T]]$
and $H=\exp(\log H)=\sum_r(\log H)^r/r!$ converges $T$-adically (only $r\le n$ contributes to
$[T^n]$); every term has $\operatorname{wt}\le0$ because $\operatorname{wt}$ is subadditive on
products. So $\operatorname{wt}(H)\le0$. A monomial $E_1^iE_2^jE_3^k$ of $[T^n]H$ then satisfies
$i+2j+3k\le n$, whence $3k\le n$. $\square$

**Remark (this is exactly the gap Day 146 named).** Day 146 §8 derived the *weak* bound
$\deg_{E_3}[T^n]H\le\lfloor2n/3\rfloor$ from $\deg_u[T^n]F_P\le2n$ and observed that (H2)
"asks for a further factor-of-two cancellation". The factor of two is the **degree collapse
under $\log$**: $\deg_u[T^n]F_P=2n$ but $\deg_u[T^n]\log F_P=n+1$ (both sharp — verified to
$T^{16}$, §7). Theorem 1 is that collapse.

### Corollary 3 — three statements lose their hypothesis
1. **Day 146 Theorem 2 is unconditional:** $F^2-F=\vartheta\,\mathcal H\,(2F-3)$, $\mathcal H=\ell_0(H)$.
   (Its proof used exactly (H2), plus Prop 1 = Day 148 Thm 4.1, which is already unconditional.)
2. **Day 148's Corollary is unconditional:**
   $\mathcal H=\dfrac{F(F-1)}{\vartheta(2F-3)}=\dfrac{2F-3}{(F-1)^2(4F-3)}=\dfrac{2G-1}{(3G-1)^2(4G-1)}\in\mathbb Z[[\vartheta]]$,
   $G=F/3$. So Day 147's "exact realizability of $(s_n)$" is a theorem, at every prime, with
   no hypotheses. **§9.2 of the Day 148 write-up should be deleted, not repaired.**
3. $\ell_0$ is a ring homomorphism on $\{\operatorname{ord}\ge0\}$, so
   $\mathcal H=\exp(\ell_0(\log H))$: the Day 146 cocycle packaging is now a corollary.

## 3. Proof A (the $E_3$-order filtration) — kept because it is a different mechanism

**Lemma ($\tau$ and the order filtration).** If $\operatorname{ord}X\ge d$ then
$\operatorname{ord}\tau X\ge d$ and $\ell_d(\tau X)=\tau'(\ell_d X)$, where $\tau'$ is $\tau$
restricted to $\mathbb Q[E_1,E_2]$, acting coefficientwise on $\mathbb Q[E_1,E_2][[\vartheta]]$.

*Proof.* $\tau(E_3^k)=\sum_{j\le k}\binom kj E_3^{\,j}(E_1+E_2+1)^{k-j}$, so
$[E_3^{\,j}T^n]\tau X=\sum_{k\ge j}\binom kj(E_1+E_2+1)^{k-j}\tau'(x_{k,n})$. A nonzero summand
needs $n-3k\ge d$, and $k\ge j$ gives $n-3j\ge d$. For $n=3j+d$ the two inequalities force
$k=j$, leaving the single term $\tau'(x_{j,3j+d})$. $\square$

**Theorem 1′ (= (H2)).** By Day 148 Thm 4.1, $\operatorname{ord}\log F_P\ge-1$; by Day 148
Cor 5.2, $\ell_{-1}(\log F_P)=\sum_kn_k\vartheta^k$ has **constant** coefficients
($b_k=(3k-1)n_k\in\mathbb Q$), so $\tau'$ fixes it. Hence
$\ell_{-1}(\log H)=\tau'(\ell_{-1}\log F_P)-\ell_{-1}(\log F_P)=0$, so
$\operatorname{ord}\log H\ge0$ and $\operatorname{ord}H=\operatorname{ord}\exp(\log H)\ge0$. $\square$

The two proofs are the same idea in the two gradings. Proof A is strictly weaker (it gives only
the $E_3$-shadow) and needs two Day 148 theorems as input; Proof B needs only the Riccati
induction. Worth keeping side by side because Proof A shows *why* the bound is sharp:
$\ell_{-1}(\log F_P)\ne0$ is $E$-free and dies, while $\ell_0(\log F_P)$ is **not** $E$-free
($\frac83E_1-8$ at $k=1$), so no further cancellation is available.

## 4. The leading symbol of $H$: one curve behind two problems

Theorem 2 says $[T^n]H$ has $u$-degree $\le n$; empirically equality holds. Call the
degree-$n$ homogeneous part $\mathcal W_n$, and $\mathcal W=\sum_n\mathcal W_nT^n
=\ell^{\mathrm{top}}_0(H)$. Since $\operatorname{wt}\le0$ elements form a ring on which
$\ell^{\mathrm{top}}_0$ is a homomorphism, and since $\tau-1=\partial+(\text{lower})$ with
$\partial:=\sum_i\partial_{u_i}$ (because $\tau=e^{\partial}$),
$$\log\mathcal W=\partial\,\Xi,\qquad \Xi:=\ell^{\mathrm{top}}_1(\log F_P) .$$

Applying $\ell^{\mathrm{top}}_1$ to (R) (the $D_1d_2$ term has $\operatorname{wt}\le1$ and so
does not reach $\operatorname{wt}=2$) gives, on the diagonal $t_i=T$, with $L_i=\theta_i\Xi$:
$$L_1=T\nu_1\nu_2,\ L_2=T\nu_1\nu_3,\ L_3=T\nu_2\nu_3,\qquad
\nu_1=u_1{+}L_1{+}L_2,\ \nu_2=u_2{+}L_1{+}L_3,\ \nu_3=u_3{+}L_2{+}L_3,$$
i.e. the **same system as Day 148 (∗)**, now with $u_i$ and an explicit $T$:
$$\boxed{\ \nu_i\bigl(1-T(e_1(\nu)-\nu_i)\bigr)=u_i\ }\qquad(i=1,2,3).$$

Summing $T\nu_i^2+(1-TP)\nu_i=u_i$ with $P:=e_1(\nu)$ gives at once
$$e_2(\nu)=\frac{P-E_1}{2T},\qquad\text{hence}\qquad \theta\,\Xi=T\,e_2(\nu)=\frac{P-E_1}{2}.$$
*(Day 148 §6 step 2, "$F=E/2$", is the case $E_1=0$ of this — there $u_i=\omega^{i-1}z$ and
$\sum\omega^{i-1}=0$. What was billed as "the mod-3 mechanism" is a general identity.)*

Solving the quadratics, $2T\nu_i=-q+\sqrt{q^2+4Tu_i}$ with $q:=1-TP$, and summing:

> **Theorem 4 (the master curve).** $q=1-T\,e_1(\nu)$ is the branch at $q=1+O(T)$ of
> $$\sum_{i=1}^{3}\sqrt{q^2+4Tu_i}\;=\;q+2 ,$$
> equivalently of the quintic (rationalise via $R_i=\sqrt{q^2+4Tu_i}$, $e_1(R)=q+2$,
> $e_2(R)=-q^2+2q+2-2TE_1$, $e_3(R)^2=\prod(q^2+4Tu_i)$):
> $$(q-1)(q+1)^3(2q+1)\;-\;2E_1T(q+1)^2(q^2-2q-2)\;-\;2T^2(q+1)\bigl[\Delta_2(q^2{+}q{+}1)+2E_1^2(q+1)\bigr]$$
> $$+\;2E_1T^3\Delta_2\,(q^2{+}2q{+}2)\;+\;16E_3T^3(q+2)^2\;-\;T^4\Delta_2^{\,2}\;=\;0,\qquad \Delta_2:=E_1^2-4E_2 .$$
> The leading symbol of $H$ is $\mathcal W=\exp(\partial\Xi)$ with $\theta\Xi=\frac{1-q}{2T}-\frac{E_1}{2}$.

**The unification.** Put $E_1=E_2=0$, $T=1$, $u_i=\omega^{i-1}z$ (so $E_3=z^3=\vartheta$) and
$q=1-2F$: the curve becomes $16E_3(3-2F)^2-16F(1-F)^3(3-4F)=0$, i.e. **exactly the Day 148
quintic** $F(1-F)^3(3-4F)=\vartheta(3-2F)^2$ (checked in `sympy`: the specialisation equals
$256\times$ the quintic). So the $b_k\equiv0\bmod3$ curve and the leading symbol of $H$ are two
specialisations of one algebraic curve; the Day 148 elimination is the general elimination.

**Narayana.** At $u_3=0$ ($E_3=0$), writing $u_1=x$, $u_2=y$: $[T^n]H$'s top part is
$(n+1)W_n$ where $W_n(x,y)=\sum_k N(n{+}1,k{+}1)x^{n-k}y^k$ is the **Narayana polynomial**,
$N(n,k)=\frac1n\binom nk\binom n{k-1}$; in particular $W_n(1,1)=C_{n+1}$ (Catalan).
*Verified for all $n\le16$ — exact match of every coefficient.* The $(n+1)$ prefactor is
Lagrange inversion: $\sum_n(n+1)W_nT^n=\frac{dY}{dT}$ with $Y=T\psi(Y)$ and, at $E_3=0$,
$\psi(Y)=1+E_1Y+E_2Y^2=\prod_{i}(1+u_iY)$. In three variables $\psi$ is no longer that
product: $\psi=1+E_1Y+E_2Y^2+2E_3Y^3+E_1E_3Y^4+2E_2E_3Y^5+(E_1E_2E_3{+}5E_3^2)Y^6+\cdots$
(every coefficient from $Y^3$ on is divisible by $E_3$). Identifying $\psi$ in closed form is
the obvious next question.

## 5. What $\Psi$ actually is: the Schur $\to$ factorial-Schur map

This came out of the hunt for the combinatorial model behind Conjecture P, and it subsumes
most of what the project has been computing since Day 110. **Rule 11 firing a second time.**

For a partition $\mu=(\mu_1\ge\mu_2\ge\mu_3\ge0)$ put $\lambda:=\mu+\rho$, $\rho=(2,1,0)$, and
define the **factorial Schur function** (rising convention)
$$\mathfrak s_\mu(u)\;:=\;\frac{\det\bigl[u_i^{(\lambda_j)}\bigr]_{i,j=1}^{3}}{V(u)},\qquad
x^{(k)}=x(x+1)\cdots(x+k-1).$$
This is Macdonald's $s_\mu(u|a)$ at $a_l=1-l$ — equivalently, up to the standard shift, the
shifted Schur function of Okounkov–Olshanski. The $\mathfrak s_\mu$ are a $\mathbb Z$-basis of
$\Lambda_3=\mathbb Z[E_1,E_2,E_3]$, and $\mathfrak s_\mu=s_\mu+(\text{lower degree})$.

> **Theorem A.** $\;\Psi^{+}(s_\mu)=\mathfrak s_\mu$ for every $\mu$, where
> $\Psi^{+}(f):=\mathcal T^{+}(fV)/V$ (so $F_P=\Psi^{+}(e^{Te_2})$; the falling-factorial
> $\Psi$ of the project is the $\varphi$-conjugate). **In words: $\Psi$ is exactly the linear
> map that replaces every Schur function by its factorial Schur counterpart.**

*Proof.* $s_\mu V=\det[u_i^{\lambda_j}]=\sum_{w}\operatorname{sgn}(w)\prod_iu_i^{\lambda_{w(i)}}$.
$\mathcal T^{+}$ is defined monomial-wise by $u^\alpha\mapsto\prod_iu_i^{(\alpha_i)}$, so it sends
this to $\sum_w\operatorname{sgn}(w)\prod_iu_i^{(\lambda_{w(i)})}=\det[u_i^{(\lambda_j)}]
=\mathfrak s_\mu V$. $\square$

> **Corollary B (closed form for $\Psi_b$ — the object of the whole project).** Since
> $e_2^{\,b}=\sum_\mu K_{\mu'(2^b)}s_\mu$ with $K$ the Kostka numbers,
> $$\boxed{\;P_b\;=\;\sum_{\mu\vdash 2b,\;\ell(\mu)\le3}K_{\mu'\,(2^b)}\;\mathfrak s_\mu\;}
> \qquad\text{hence}\qquad
> F_P=\sum_{\substack{\ell(\mu)\le3\\ |\mu|\ \mathrm{even}}}\frac{K_{\mu'(2^{|\mu|/2})}}{(|\mu|/2)!}\,T^{|\mu|/2}\,\mathfrak s_\mu .$$
> *Verified against the $\Psi$-recursion of `core.py` for all $b\le6$: e.g.
> $P_3=\mathfrak s_{222}+2\mathfrak s_{321}+\mathfrak s_{330}$,
> $P_5=5\mathfrak s_{433}+6\mathfrak s_{442}+5\mathfrak s_{532}+4\mathfrak s_{541}+\mathfrak s_{550}$.*

> **Theorem C (what $\tau$ is).** $\;\tau(\mathfrak s_\mu)=\mathfrak s_{\mu+(1,1,1)}/E_3$,
> equivalently $$\Psi^{+}(e_3\,f)\;=\;E_3\;\tau\bigl(\Psi^{+}(f)\bigr)\quad\text{for all }f\in\Lambda_3 .$$
> **So under $\Psi$, the shift $\tau$ is just multiplication by $e_3$.**

*Proof.* $(x+1)^{(k)}=x^{(k+1)}/x$, so
$\det[(u_i+1)^{(\lambda_j)}]=\frac1{E_3}\det[u_i^{(\lambda_j+1)}]=\frac1{E_3}\mathfrak s_{\mu+(1^3)}V$,
while $V(u+1)=V(u)$. And $e_3s_\mu=s_{\mu+(1^3)}$ in three variables. $\square$

> **Corollary D.** $\;H=\dfrac{\Psi^{+}(e_3\,e^{Te_2})}{E_3\,\Psi^{+}(e^{Te_2})}$ — Day 148 §9.3's
> "moment ratio", now with a two-line proof, and with both sides expanded explicitly:
> $$H=\frac{\sum_b\frac{T^b}{b!}\sum_\mu K_{\mu'(2^b)}\,\mathfrak s_{\mu+(1^3)}}
> {E_3\sum_b\frac{T^b}{b!}\sum_\mu K_{\mu'(2^b)}\,\mathfrak s_\mu}.$$

> **Corollary E (the $\Psi$-recursion is the Pieri rule).** Conjugating multiplication by $e_2$
> through $\Psi^{+}$ gives the difference operator
> $$\mathcal B:=\Psi^{+}\circ(e_2\cdot)\circ(\Psi^{+})^{-1}
> =\frac1{V}\,e_2\bigl(u_1S_1,u_2S_2,u_3S_3\bigr)\,V,\qquad S_i:u_i\mapsto u_i+1,$$
> i.e. $\mathcal B(f)=\sum_{i<j}u_iu_j\,f(u+e_i+e_j)\,\frac{V(u+e_i+e_j)}{V(u)}$, and
> $P_b=\mathcal B^b(1)$. In the factorial-Schur basis $\mathcal B$ is **exactly the $e_2$-Pieri
> rule**, all structure constants $0$ or $1$:
> $$\mathcal B(\mathfrak s_\mu)=\sum_{\nu/\mu\ \text{a vertical 2-strip}}\mathfrak s_\nu .$$

*Direct proof of the Pieri form, without Theorem A.* The operators $\hat u_i:=u_iS_i$ commute and
act on rising factorials by raising the index, $\hat u\,x^{(k)}=x\,(x+1)^{(k)}=x^{(k+1)}$. Since
$\prod_i(1+z\hat u_i)$ applies the *same* linear map $1+zR$ ($R:x^{(k)}\mapsto x^{(k+1)}$) in every
variable, and the determinant is multilinear in its columns,
$$\sum_{k}z^k\,e_k(\hat u)\,\det[u_i^{(\lambda_j)}]
=\det\bigl[u_i^{(\lambda_j)}+z\,u_i^{(\lambda_j+1)}\bigr]
=\sum_{S\subseteq\{1,2,3\}}z^{|S|}\det\bigl[u_i^{(\lambda_j+[j\in S])}\bigr].$$
Comparing coefficients of $z^2$ and subtracting $\rho$: $\lambda\mapsto\lambda+\mathbb 1_S$ with
$|S|=2$ is $\mu\mapsto\mu+\mathbb 1_S$, one box in each of two distinct rows — a vertical
$2$-strip; if the result is not a partition two $\lambda$'s coincide and the determinant vanishes,
which is precisely the Pieri condition. $\square$

**The $e_k$-family, and the cleanest form of (H1).** The same generating-function computation
gives, for every $k$, that $\mathcal B_k:=V^{-1}e_k(\hat u)V$ is the $e_k$-Pieri operator
($\mathcal B_k\mathfrak s_\mu=\sum_{\nu/\mu\ \text{vertical }k\text{-strip}}\mathfrak s_\nu$), and
at $k=3$ this is an identity of operators, not just a Pieri rule:
$$e_3(\hat u)=\hat u_1\hat u_2\hat u_3=E_3\,S_1S_2S_3=E_3\,\tau .$$
So **$\tau$ is the $e_3$-Pieri operator divided by $E_3$**. Since $\mathcal B_2$ and $\mathcal B_3$
are both polynomials in the commuting $\hat u_i$ they commute, and with
$F_P=\Psi^{+}(e^{Te_2})=e^{T\mathcal B_2}(1)$, $\mathcal B_3(1)=\mathfrak s_{(1,1,1)}=E_3$:
$$\boxed{\;H=\frac{e^{T\mathcal B_2}(E_3)}{E_3\;e^{T\mathcal B_2}(1)}\;}$$
— (H1) is the statement that this ratio of two Pieri-flows, started at $E_3$ and at $1$, has
integral coefficients. Both flows are $0/1$-combinatorial; the only source of denominators is the
$1/b!$ in $e^{T\mathcal B_2}$.

## 6. (H1): what is now known, and what is dead

### 6.1 The $\tau$-shift is insertion of one cubic polynomial (new, exact)

Because $\partial_x^{\,n}x^u|_{x=1}=(u)_n$, the umbral map is
$\mathcal T(h)=\bigl[h(\partial_x)\,x^u\bigr]_{x=1}$, $x^u=\prod x_i^{u_i}$. Shifting
$u\mapsto u+1$ multiplies $x^u$ by $x_1x_2x_3$, and $[G(\partial),x_k]=(\partial_{\xi_k}G)(\partial)$,
so evaluation at $x=1$ turns the shift into $D:=\prod_k(1+\partial_{\xi_k})$ on the symbol.
For $G:=e^{Te_2(\xi)}V(\xi)$ the three operators
$A_k:=1+T(e_1(\xi)-\xi_k)+\partial_{\xi_k}$ **commute** ($[\partial_j,e_1-\xi_k]=1-\delta_{jk}$
cancels against $[e_1-\xi_j,\partial_k]$), $A_1A_2A_3V$ is antisymmetric hence divisible by $V$,
and one computes:

> **Theorem 5.** $\displaystyle \prod_{k=1}^3\bigl(1+\partial_{\xi_k}\bigr)\Bigl[e^{Te_2(\xi)}V(\xi)\Bigr]
> \;=\;g(\xi,T)\;e^{Te_2(\xi)}V(\xi)$, with
> $$g=1+2\bigl(e_1+3\bigr)T+\bigl(e_1^2+4e_1+e_2\bigr)T^2+\bigl(e_1e_2-e_3\bigr)T^3 .$$
> Consequently, with $\Phi:=\mathcal T(e^{Te_2}V)/V=\varphi(F_P)$,
> $$\frac{\Phi(u+1)}{\Phi(u)}=\frac{\Psi\bigl(g\,e^{Te_2}\bigr)}{\Psi\bigl(e^{Te_2}\bigr)},
> \qquad\text{and}\qquad \text{(H1)}\iff \frac{\Phi(u+1)}{\Phi(u)}\in\mathbb Z[E][[T]]$$
> Indeed $F_P(u)=\Phi(-u)$, so with $\mathcal R(u):=\Phi(u+1)/\Phi(u)$ one has
> $$H(u)=\mathcal R(-u-1)^{-1},$$
> and $u\mapsto-u-1$ is the substitution $E_i\mapsto(-1)^i\tau(E_i)$ — a ring automorphism of
> $\mathbb Z[E]$ (it is $\varphi$ followed by $\tau$; **not** $\tau$ followed by $\varphi$).
> *Both verified symbolically: the $g$-identity to $T^7$, the bridge $H=\mathcal R(-u-1)^{-1}$ to $T^8$.*

Equivalently, since $\mathcal T(\xi_kh)(u)=u_k\,\mathcal T(h)(u-e_k)$, the substitution
$\xi_k\mapsto u_kS_k^{-1}$ turns $g$ into a **difference operator with polynomial coefficients**:
$\tau(\Phi V)=g^{\vee}(\Phi V)$ — an explicit finite contiguous relation for the whole family.

### 6.2 Conjecture P (positivity) — strictly stronger than (H1)

> **Conjecture P.** $[T^n]H\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}[E_1,E_2,E_3]$ for every $n$; moreover every
> coefficient is $\ge n+1$, with the minimum attained at $E_1^n$ (whose coefficient is exactly
> $n+1$). Also $P_b\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}[E_1,E_2,E_3]$ with minimal coefficient $1$.

**Verified:** $H$ for all $n\le16$ (204 monomials at $n=16$, zero negatives, min $=n+1$ each
time); $P_b$ for all $b\le20$ (946 monomials at $b=20$, zero negatives). Conjecture P
**implies (H1)** and would make it combinatorial. The Narayana/Catalan appearance in §4 is
evidence that the counting objects are of Schröder/tree type — which is where the Day 143
geode thread was pointing.

Signature data for the model hunt: $P_b(0,0,0)=(b!)^2$, $P_b(1,0,0)=b!(b+1)!$,
$P_b(0,1,0)=\prod_{j\le b}(j^2+1)$ (all three are the ${}_2F_0$ slice: $P_b=U^{(b)}V^{(b)}$
with $E_1=U+V-2$, $E_2=(U-1)(V-1)$), $[E_1^b]P_b=b!$, $[E_2^b]P_b=1$;
$H_n(0,0,0)=1,6,39,288,2404,22464,232908,2659008,\dots$

### 6.3 What the closed form does give at once

$\frac{\prod_i(u_i)_{m_i}}{a!b!c!}=\kappa(a,b,c)\,a!\,b!\,c!\prod_i\binom{u_i}{m_i}$ with
$\kappa=\binom{a+b}{a}\binom{a+c}{a}\binom{b+c}{b}$, so
$$[T^n]\bigl(\Phi V\bigr)=\sum_{a+b+c=n}\kappa\,a!b!c!\;\prod_i\binom{u_i}{m_i}\;V(u-m)
\ \in\ \mathrm{Int}(\mathbb Z^3),$$
i.e. $\Phi V$ is an **integer-valued** polynomial in $u$ for every $T$-degree. Since
$V(u\pm1)=V(u)$, this gives $\frac{\Phi(u+1)}{\Phi(u)}\in\mathbb Z_\ell[[T]]$ for every
$u\in\mathbb Z_\ell^3$ with $\ell\nmid V(u)$, hence: $[T^n]H$ is $\ell$-integral at every
$E\in\mathbb F_\ell^3$ coming from a cubic that splits with distinct roots mod $\ell$
($\binom\ell3$ residue classes). By Schwartz–Zippel a nonzero polynomial of total degree
$\le n$ (Theorem 2) cannot vanish on $\binom\ell3$ points once $\binom\ell3>n\ell^2$:

> **Proposition 6 (partial (H1)).** For every prime $\ell$ and every $n$ with
> $(\ell-1)(\ell-2)>6n\ell$ — in particular for all $\ell\gtrsim 6n$ — one has
> $[T^n]H\in\mathbb Z_{(\ell)}[E_1,E_2,E_3]$.

This is genuinely weak (each prime only controls finitely many coefficients) and it cannot be
improved by this route, for the structural reason in §5.4.

### 6.4 Why the integer-valued route stalls, precisely

* Split points only see $\binom\ell3$ residue classes, and $\binom\ell3/\ell^3\to1/6$, so the
  minimal degree of a polynomial vanishing on them is $\Theta(\ell)$ — Proposition 6 is optimal
  for this method. At $\ell=2$ three integers are never distinct mod $2$: **no information at all**.
* At $\ell=3$ the split locus is the single class $E\equiv(0,2,0)$, so all one gets is
  $3$-integrality *on that class* — which does not exclude e.g. a denominator $E_3/3$.
* One cannot escape to non-split points by base change: $\binom{x}{m}$ does **not** map
  $O\to O$ for $O$ unramified over $\mathbb Z_\ell$ (e.g. $\binom{\zeta_3}{2}=\zeta_3(\zeta_3-1)/2\notin\mathbb Z_2[\zeta_3]$).

### 6.5 Two things killed this session

* **Conjecture C is FALSE.** "$[T^n]F_P$ is $\ell$-integral whenever the cubic is separable
  mod $\ell$" — this would have given (H1) immediately (Zariski density over all
  $\mathbb F_{\ell^f}$ then upgrades pointwise integrality to $\mathbb Z_\ell[E]$). Tested over
  14835 (integer $E$, $\ell$) pairs with $\ell\nmid\operatorname{disc}$: **38108 violations**,
  the first at $E=(-22,6,24)$, $\ell=5$, $n=5$ ($v_5(\Psi_5)=0<v_5(5!)=1$). So the $b!$
  cancellation is genuinely a property of the **ratio**, not of $F_P$.
* **$H$ is not a Stieltjes/J-fraction object in $T$.** A continued fraction with non-negative
  integral coefficients would have delivered Conjecture P and (H1) at one stroke. The J-fraction
  coefficients of $\sum H_nT^n$ at $E=(0,0,0)$ are $b=(6,63,-\frac{36253}{89},\dots)$,
  $\lambda=(3,-\frac{89}{3},\dots)$: not integral, not positive. Dead.

## 7. Audit of the Day 148 write-up

**One real defect, in §5 Step 3** ("the prefactor is invisible"). The text argues that the
correction terms in $\mathcal R=e^{-S}V(M)e^{S}$ "are $\Delta$-derivatives of already-produced
factors, hence of order $\ge-1$ instead of $-2$", and concludes
$\ell_{-3}(\mathcal R)=V(\nu)$ exactly. **The parenthetical is wrong**: the produced factors

> A correction term replaces one factor by $\Delta_{kl}\Delta_{ij}S$, whose $\ell_{-1}$ is a
> $\theta$-difference of $\ell_{-1}(S)$, hence $O(z^2)$ (because $L_i=\hat t_i\nu_j\nu_k$ and
> $\nu_i=\omega^{i-1}z+O(z^2)$); the two surviving factors are $O(z)$ each. So every correction
> is $O(z^4)$, while $V(\nu)=Dz^3+O(z^4)$. Therefore
> $\ell_{-3}(\mathcal R)=V(\nu)+O(z^4)=Dz^3(1+O(z))$, so $\ell_0(\mathcal R/V(u))$ is a unit
> of $\mathcal K[[z]]$ congruent to $1$ mod $z$, $\operatorname{ord}(\mathcal R/V(u))\ge0$, and
> $\log(\mathcal R/V(u))$ has order $\ge0$ — which is all Step 3 is used for.

## 9. Where this leaves (H1)

It is now a completely explicit question about Kostka numbers and factorial Schur functions,
with no $p$-adics anywhere:
$$\text{(H1)}\iff
\frac{\sum_b\frac{T^b}{b!}\sum_\mu K_{\mu'(2^b)}\,\mathfrak s_{\mu+(1^3)}}
{\sum_b\frac{T^b}{b!}\sum_\mu K_{\mu'(2^b)}\,\mathfrak s_\mu}\ \in\ E_3\,\mathbb Z[E][[T]] .$$
The whole difficulty is the $b!$: both numerator and denominator are Hurwitz series with
coefficients in $\mathbb Z[E]$, and the claim is that the quotient is an *ordinary* series.

Three concrete next steps, in order of promise:

1. **Prove Conjecture P.** It implies (H1). It is now the statement that the Kostka-weighted
   sum $\sum_\mu K_{\mu'(2^b)}\mathfrak s_\mu$ is $E$-positive although the individual
   $\mathfrak s_\mu$ are not — so the mechanism is a cancellation *between* factorial Schur
   functions, and the right move is to find the basis in which it is manifest.
   (Signature data for the model: $P_b(0,0,0)=(b!)^2$, $P_b(1,0,0)=b!(b+1)!$, $[E_1^b]P_b=b!$.)
2. **Attack the $b!$ directly in the Pieri picture.** $P_b=\mathcal B^b(1)$ with $\mathcal B$ the
   $e_2$-Pieri operator, so $F_P=\sum_b\mathcal B^b(1)T^b/b!=\exp(T\mathcal B)(1)$ and
   $E_3\,\tau F_P=\Psi^{+}(e_3e^{Te_2})$. (H1) is then a statement about
   $\exp(T\mathcal B)$ conjugated by multiplication by $e_3$ — i.e. about the commutator of
   $e_2$-Pieri and $e_3$-multiplication, which is $0$ in $\Lambda_3$ but not after the
   $\Psi$-twist. That commutator is the entire content of (H1) and it is a finite,
   combinatorial object.
3. **Identify the Lagrange kernel $\psi$** of §4 in closed form; it is the leading symbol's
   combinatorial certificate and would confirm the tree model behind the Narayana numbers.
