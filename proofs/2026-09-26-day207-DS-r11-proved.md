This is the first length-3 instance of DS that is a theorem.

## 0. Statement

The conventions are those of `scripts/day198/p2Y_er.py::build_action`, which are the same as Day 205 §1 and Day 206b §0.
- s := q^{-1}.
- [n] := [n]_t, with [n] = 0 for n ≤ 0.
- e_μ is the ordinary product of the e_{μ_i}(X_1..X_m).
- ⋆ is Hikita's product (2503.23597, Def. 3.4). Define C_r := e_1⋆e_1⋆e_r.

**DS(r,1,1)** is the length-3 slice of DS-triangular (Day 196 §2) at λ = (r,1,1). It asserts two things:
- C_r lies in the span of e_μ for μ ⪰ (r,1,1), μ ⊢ r+2. For r ≥ 2 these μ are exactly (r+2), (r+1,1), (r,2) and (r,1,1).
- The coefficient of e_{(r,1,1)} is q^{-n((r,1,1))} = q^{-3}, and every other coefficient vanishes at q = 1.

**Theorem.** For every r ≥ 1 and m ≥ 1, the following identity holds in Λ_m:

  C_r = s³ e_{(r,1,1)} + s²(1−s)[2] e_{(r,2)} + s(1−s)([r+1] + t[r] + s) e_{(r+1,1)} + (1−s)²[r+2]([r+1] + s) e_{(r+2)}.

Now take r ≥ 2 and m ≥ r+2. The four e_μ are then distinct and linearly independent, so this is *the* e-expansion. In particular:
- The support is exactly the DS-interval of (r,1,1). All four coefficients are nonzero.
- The leading coefficient is s³ = q^{-3}.
- The three off-diagonal coefficients carry a factor (1−s), so they vanish at q = 1.

Hence **DS(r,1,1) holds, in its sharpened triangular form, for all r ≥ 2.** It also holds in the stable limit.

At r = 2 the formula reproduces the Day 196/198 table: q^{-3}, (q−1)(t+1)/q³, (q−1)(2qt²+2qt+q+1)/q³ and (q−1)²(t+1)(t²+1)(qt²+qt+q+1)/q³.

## 1. Proof (direct route)

**Step 0: ⋆ is e_1(Y)•.** This is Day 205 R0. Y_i•1 = X_i, so e_1(Y)•1 = e_1 and 𝔮^{-1}(e_1) = e_1(Y). For any G, e_1⋆G = (e_1(Y)𝔮^{-1}G)•1 = e_1(Y)•G. Applying this twice:

  C_r = e_1⋆(e_1⋆e_r) = e_1(Y)•(e_1(Y)•e_r).

This uses Hikita Def. 3.4 and the bijectivity of 𝔮_(m) (cited; the source is at verified-quote). ⋆ is transported from the commutative product on Λ(Y), so it is associative and commutative, and the order of the factors does not matter.

**Step 1: Thm 3.12 in operator form, at r and at r+1.** For all n ≥ 1:

  e_1(Y)•e_n = (1−s)[n+1] e_{n+1} + s e_1e_n.   (T_n)

*Proof, from scratch.* Day 205 Step A gives e_1(Y) = σ_mπ on symmetric F. Day 205 Step B gives π(e_n) = X_1e_n(tail) + sX_1²e_{n−1}(tail). Day 205b §3 gives:
- σ_m[X_1e_n(tail)] = M_1(n)/(1−t) = [n+1]e_{n+1};
- σ_m[X_1²e_{n−1}(tail)] = M_2(n−1)/(1−t) = −[n+1]e_{n+1} + e_1e_n.

Adding these with weights 1 and s gives (T_n). ∎ This is Hikita's Thm 3.12, re-derived. It is also the "anchor" check in Day 205 `reduction_0` (e).

Applying (T_r):

  C_r = (1−s)[r+1] · e_1(Y)•e_{r+1} + s · e_1(Y)•(e_1e_r).   (1)

**Step 2: the two pieces.**
- (T_{r+1}) gives e_1(Y)•e_{r+1} = (1−s)[r+2] e_{r+2} + s e_{(r+1,1)}.
- Sub-Lemma Z (operator form) is proved in Day 205 Steps A–D together with Day 205b (L1)–(L4). It holds for all r ≥ 1 and m ≥ 1:

  e_1(Y)•(e_1e_r) = (1−s)²[r+2] e_{r+2} + (1−s)(t[r] + s) e_{(r+1,1)} + s(1−s)[2] e_{(r,2)} + s² e_{(r,1,1)}.

  (Day 205b writes this as (q−1)(qt[r]+1)/q² for the (r+1,1) coefficient, which is the same thing.)

**Step 3: assembly.** Substitute both pieces into (1) and collect terms:

| μ | from (1−s)[r+1]·(T_{r+1}) | from s·Z | total |
|---|---|---|---|
| (r+2) | (1−s)²[r+1][r+2] | s(1−s)²[r+2] | (1−s)²[r+2]([r+1]+s) |
| (r+1,1) | s(1−s)[r+1] | s(1−s)(t[r]+s) | s(1−s)([r+1]+t[r]+s) |
| (r,2) | 0 | s²(1−s)[2] | s²(1−s)[2] |
| (r,1,1) | 0 | s³ | s³ |

This proves the identity for every m.

**Uniqueness.** Take r ≥ 2 and m ≥ r+2. Then (r+2), (r+1,1), (r,2) and (r,1,1) are distinct partitions with largest part ≤ m. Since e_1, …, e_m are algebraically independent (Macdonald I (2.4)), the e_μ are linearly independent.

**Nonvanishing.** At q = 2 and t = 1, the four coefficients are 1/8, 1/4, (1/4)(r+1+r+1/2) and (1/4)(r+2)(r+3/2). All are nonzero. ∎

### Why this is not the Day 195/198 tautology

The Day 198 note (§6) found that iterating Thm 3.12 collapses to C = C. The reason was that A = e_1⋆(e_1e_r) was an unknown. Sub-Lemma Z computes A independently, from the level-1 action, and that is what breaks the circle. The route uses only e_1(Y). It does not use p_2(Y), Newton, W_r or τ_r.

## 2. Second proof (Day 198 route, now also closed)

Newton's identity e_1(Y)² = p_2(Y) + 2e_2(Y) holds in the commutative ring Λ(Y). Acting on e_r gives:

  C_r = p_2(Y)•e_r + 2 e_2(Y)•e_r = p_2(Y)•e_r + 2t W_r.

The inputs are:
- Lemma 1 (p_2(Y)-Pieri, including τ_r), proved as Day 206b §6;
- W_r = t^{-1}e_2(Y)•e_r, proved in Day 206b.

Support and leading coefficient then follow as in Day 198 §5:
- p_2(Y)•e_r is supported on the DS-interval, and its (r,1,1)-coefficient is q^{-3};
- W_r lives on the two-row interval, so its (r,1,1)-coefficient is 0.

`ds_r11_route_consistency.py` checks both routes against the closed form symbolically in u = t^r, which covers all r at once. All four coefficients agree (Δ = 0).

Route 1 is preferred because it has fewer inputs. The only input it shares with Route 2 is the Day 205/205b machinery.

On the question file: the D in Day 198's (⋆) is D = e_2⋆e_2 = W_2. It is not an "e_1⋆(e_1⋆e_2)-type" term. Sub-Lemma Z enters Route 1 directly, and enters Route 2 only through Day 206b.
