\section*{Verdict}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{Your hardest question: yes, $B'$ is coaction-closed.} The reason is sharper than
  ``$\lambda,\rho$ fix $g$'', and it shows that constancy plays no role (below). The explicit coactions are
  \[
    \lambda(s,g)=\bigl(s,\ a\mapsto (a,g)\bigr),\quad \lambda^\sharp\bigl(a,(a',t')\bigr)=(a',t');\qquad
    \rho(s,g)=\bigl((s,g),\ (a,t')\mapsto t'\bigr),\quad \rho^\sharp\bigl((a,t'),t''\bigr)=(a,t'').
  \]
  So $\lambda$ fixes $g$ but \emph{moves} $s$ to every $a\in S$, and $\rho$ fixes the whole position.
  A set of positions is closed iff it is $S$-saturated, i.e.\ of the form $S\times G$. Since
  $B'=S\times\cst(T)$, it is closed. \textbf{checked-sober} (by hand, and by brute force at all nine
  shapes $n,m\in\{1,2,3\}$; all five bicomodule laws hold on $B$ and on $B'$).
\item \textbf{But closure does not single out $B'$.} Every $S\times G$, $G\subseteq T^S$, is a
  sub-bicomodule, the non-constant part $S\times\mathrm{NC}$ included. Each slice $B_g=S\times\{g\}$ is a minimal
  closed set, the structure maps above do not depend on $g$, and so $B\cong T^S\cdot B_{g_0}$ as bicomodules.
  Any injection $T\hookrightarrow T^S$ gives a bicomodule iso from $A$ onto its image (checked with $m$
  non-constant maps). $\cst$ is canonical for a different reason: it is the \emph{unique natural} map
  $T\to T^S$ (Yoneda: $\mathrm{Nat}(\mathrm{Hom}(1,-),\mathrm{Hom}(S,-))\cong\mathrm{Hom}(S,1)=1$). Say so (S1).
\item \textbf{Clause (2), Thm 5: correct as a statement about $\bar F_A\subseteq\bar F_B$}, and the
  verdict sentence is wrong at $(1,2)$, $(1,3)$ (M2).
\item \textbf{Clause (3), Thm 6: the counts and the refutation are correct.} However, $e(s,g)=(s,\cst_{g(s)})$ is \emph{not} a
  bicomodule map. It fails $\lambda$-equivariance at every non-constant $g$, i.e.\ on exactly your interesting
  locus (M1). The repair is cheap and keeps every number.
\item \textbf{MUST: 2. SHOULD: 3. NIT: 2.} M1--M3 of UID 405: all three addressed. M3 is fully landed,
  M1 is landed apart from the verdict sentence (new M2), and M2 is landed apart from the retract's type (new M1).
\end{itemize}

\section*{Findings}
\subsection*{MUST}
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{M\arabic*.},leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{\S6 opening, Thm 6, Thm 7: $e$ is not a bicomodule retraction.} By hand:
  \[
    \lambda(e(s,g))=\bigl(s,\ a\mapsto(a,\cst_{g(s)})\bigr),\qquad
    (\St_S\tri e)(\lambda(s,g))=\bigl(s,\ a\mapsto e(a,g)=(a,\cst_{g(a)})\bigr).
  \]
  These differ as soon as $g(a)\neq g(s)$ for some $a$, i.e.\ for every non-constant $g$. The script finds the first
  failure at $(2,2)$, position $(0,(a,b))$, and fails likewise at $(2,3),(3,2),(3,3)$. $e$ is $\rho$-equivariant. ``Evaluate at
  the \emph{current} input'' is what breaks it, because $\lambda$ changes the current input. Also,
  ``the retract $T^S\twoheadrightarrow\cst(T)$'' is ill-typed: $e$ needs $s$, so it is a map
  $S\times T^S\to S\times T^S$, not a map $T^S\to T$.\\
  \emph{Fix.} Either (a) say explicitly that $e$ is a retraction of position sets only and drop ``the worker
  arises from the weld by'' it. Or (b) use $r_{s_0}(s,g)=(s,\cst_{g(s_0)})$, $r^\sharp=\mathrm{id}$, for a
  chosen base input $s_0$. This one \emph{is} a bicomodule retraction $B\twoheadrightarrow B'$ (checked, all shapes). It
  forgets $g|_{S\setminus\{s_0\}}$, with fibre $m^{n-1}$, so every number in (ii)--(iii) survives with $s\to s_0$.
  It is honestly non-canonical: natural maps $(-)^S\Rightarrow\mathrm{id}$ correspond to elements of $S$
  (Yoneda), so there is \emph{no} choice-free bicomodule retraction when $n\ge2$. On $\bar F$, which you
  already evaluate at a fixed $\St_S$-object, $e$ and $r_{s_0}$ agree. This is probably what you meant.
\item \textbf{p.\,6, ``Verdict for clause (2)'', and the email's ``worker = content iff $|S|=1\vee|T|=1$''.}
  Your Thm 5 makes the content factor the power $V\mapsto V^S$. Then $\bar F_A=T\cdot V^S$ equals the content iff
  $m=1$. At $(1,2)$ we have $A=B$ on the nose, but $\bar F_A=2V\neq V$. So the ``true when $|S|=1$'' half
  is false as literally stated. What Thm 5 proves is \emph{worker $=$ whole weld} ($\bar F_A=\bar F_B$, and $A=B$)
  iff $n=1\vee m=1$. Under the literal reading, clause (2) (\emph{worker $=$ content factor}) holds iff $m=1$,
  and fails at $(1,2)$, $(1,3)$ as well as on $n,m\ge2$. Pick one reading and state it. I share the blame here:
  my UID 405 M1 wrote ``$B=A$ on the nose, yet Theorem 6 says the worker is not the content'', which runs the
  two readings together.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{SHOULD}

\item \textbf{Lemma 3 and Def.~3 framing.} The proof never uses constancy, so state the general fact: the
  sub-bicomodules supported on position subsets are exactly $S\times G$, and $B\cong\coprod_{g\in T^S}B_g$
  with all $B_g$ isomorphic. Then ``$B'$ is canonical'' rests on Yoneda (diagonal $=$ unique natural $T\to
  T^S$), not on closure. Positive control in the script: $S\times\mathrm{NC}$ is closed at every shape.
\item \textbf{Prop.~1 is circular as written, and Remark 2's caveat can go.} Thm 1 \emph{defines} $A$'s
  structure by transport along $\sigma$, which makes $\sigma$ an iso trivially. The non-trivial statement uses the
  cobase-change structure of Remark 2, which takes three lines. Take the cofunctors
  $\pi_S(s,t)=s$, $\pi_S^\sharp(a)=(a,t)$ and $\pi_T(s,t)=t$, $\pi_T^\sharp(t')=(s,t')$. Then
  $\lambda_A=(\pi_S\tri A)\delta_A$ and $\rho_A=(A\tri\pi_T)\delta_A$ give
  $\lambda_A(s,t)=(s,a\mapsto(a,t))$, $\lambda_A^\sharp(a,(a',t'))=(a',t')$,
  $\rho_A(s,t)=((s,t),(a,t')\mapsto t')$ and $\rho_A^\sharp((a,t'),t'')=(a,t'')$.
  Apply $\sigma$ and you get the displayed $\lambda,\rho$ of $B'$ with $g=\cst_t$, since
  $\lambda_B(s,\cst_t)=(s,a\mapsto\sigma(a,t))$. So $\sigma$ is a bicomodule iso from the
  \emph{intrinsic} structure. All five laws for $A$ and both equivariances of $\sigma$ are computed at all nine shapes,
  and $\bar F_A(V)=T\cdot V^S$ is recomputed from $A$'s own structure, not via $\sigma$.
\item \textbf{Lemma 3 proof text.} (a) The $\rho$ bullet: in $B\tri\St_T$ the copy of $B$ is \emph{outer}. $\rho$
  keeps $(s,g)$ entirely and labels direction $(a,t')$ by $t'$. (b) ``$\delta_T(t,\psi)$'': positions of $\St_T$
  are bare $t\in T$, and there is no $\psi$. Likewise $\delta_S(s,\varphi)$. (c) The $\lambda$ bullet should say that
  $\lambda$ moves $s$, which is why the closure needs $S$-saturation and not just ``$g$ fixed''. My negative control NEG1
  is exactly this: $\{(s_0,\cst_t)\}$ is not closed once $n\ge2$.
\end{enumerate}
