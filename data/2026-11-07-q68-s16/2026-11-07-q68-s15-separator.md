# Q68 at S_15: the (★)-violator ⇒ twin hunch meets its first separator (PROVE 281, 2026-11-07)

Scripts and outputs are in `proofs/prove281/`. Conventions, Lemma A, (F1)–(F3), C1–C4 are in
`proofs/2026-11-06-q68-kl-cone-twins.md`. Lemma S and the Step-2 y-reduction are in `proofs/2026-11-06-q68c-s14-twin.md`.

## Problem

Use GW convention C (arXiv 2604.18894, Ex. 4.7). Left cells of S_n are {Q fixed}, and L(w) = Des P(w).
A *candidate pair* in shape λ is (P_x, P_z) ∈ SYT(λ)² with Des P_x = A and ∅ ≠ Des P_z ⊆ int A
(this is the C2 necessary condition). A *(★)-violation* is a (λ, A) that carries at least one candidate pair.
Registry hunch `Q68-typeA-star-iff-twin`: every (★)-violating (λ, A) is a dominated twin.

**Task.** List all violations at n = 15 and decide every candidate pair.

## Answer

**The hunch is DEAD at n = 15, as registered at the (λ, A) level.** It survives only in its weaker, shape-level form.

| | n = 14 | n = 15 |
|---|---|---|
| shapes carrying a violation | 4 | 9 |
| exact (λ, A) violations | 4 | 34 |
| candidate pairs (P_x, P_z) | 4 | 48 |
| dominated twins | 4 | **26** |
| (λ, A) with no twin | 0 | **10** |
| shapes with no twin | 0 | **0** |

The 22 failures have two causes:

- **20 fail a parity condition**, Lemma P below. This condition is necessary, it is dumb, and nobody had written it down.
  At n = 14 the parity was automatically right, which is why 4/4 looked like evidence.
- **2 fail on a genuine below-x obstruction**, Proposition U. The two form one orbit under the diagram automorphism.
  This is the case PROVE 281 warned about: an element y < x of the cell with L(y) ⊋ L(x). The "4-row argument" of PROVE 280
  Step 2 does not cover it, even though the shape (5,4,3,3) still has only 4 rows. The long first row is what makes room for Des ⊋ A.

**Revised picture (n ≤ 15, computed).** A candidate pair is a twin iff it passes parity and has no U-obstruction.
At the shape level, a shape has a twin iff it carries a violation. That holds for 9/9 shapes at n = 15.

## 2. Two necessary conditions

**Lemma P (parity).** Let W be simply laced and connected, and let (x, z) be a dominated twin. Then l(x) ≡ l(z) (mod 2).

*Proof.* By C2, ∅ ≠ L(z) ⊆ L(x), so L(x) ≠ ∅. Also L(x) ≠ S, because w_0 forms a singleton cell. Since the diagram is
connected, there are adjacent s ∈ L(x) and t ∉ L(x), with m(s,t) = 3. By C1 and (F2), x′ = *x ∈ Γ. Also
μ(x′, x) = 1, and t ∈ L(x′) \ L(x). The twin hypothesis therefore gives μ(x′, z) ≥ 1. A nonzero μ needs an odd length
gap, and l(x′) = l(x) ± 1, so l(z) ≡ l(x). ∎

In type A, the parity of l(x) − l(z) for x = (P_x, Q) and z = (P_z, Q) does not depend on Q. A right star changes each
length by ±1, and right stars connect all cells of the shape (§4). So Lemma P is a condition on (P_x, P_z) alone.
(Reifegerste's sign formula for RSK would give this directly, but that is recall-only and not used.)

**Proposition U (the below-x obstruction; certificate-proved).** Take λ = (5,4,3,3), A = {2,3,4,6,7,8,10,11,12}, and

    P_x = [[1,2,6,10,15],[3,7,11,14],[4,8,12],[5,9,13]]
    P_z = [[1,2,3,7,15],[4,5,6,11],[8,9,10],[12,13,14]]      Des P_z = {3,7,11} = int A,
    P_y = [[1,2,6,10,14],[3,7,11,15],[4,8,12],[5,9,13]]      Des P_y = A ∪ {14}.

Then (x, z) passes parity, every star image s·x satisfies μ(sx, z) = 1, and yet (x, z) is **not** a twin, in any cell.
In the superstandard cell Q_0 = [[1..5],[6..9],[10..12],[13..15]] the elements are:
- x = [5,9,13,14,15,1,4,8,12,3,7,11,2,6,10], with l(x) = 59;
- y = [1,5,9,13,15,4,8,12,14,3,7,11,2,6,10], with l(y) = 52, so y < x;
- z, with l(z) = 77.

Then:
- **μ(y, x) = 1.** The left-star path (1,2,3,5,6,7,12) ends at a Bruhat pair with gap 1 (Lemma S).
- **μ(y, z) = 0.** No left star applies to (y, z) simultaneously, because the left orbit is a single point. The right-star path
  (4,3,2,1,5,4,3,6,5,7) ends at a Bruhat-incomparable pair. Right stars preserve μ by Lemma S applied to inverses, with
  P_{a,b} = P_{a⁻¹,b⁻¹}. That identity holds because the anti-involution T_w ↦ T_{w⁻¹} of the Hecke algebra commutes with
  the bar involution and preserves the degree bounds, so it sends C′_w to C′_{w⁻¹}.
  Independently, `undecQ.py` finds y and z incomparable in 31150 of the 75075 cells of the shape.

So y ∈ Γ, L(y) ⊄ L(x), and μ(y, z) = 0 < 1 = μ(y, x). Lemma A's hypothesis fails. A dominated twin is *defined* by that hypothesis,
so (x, z) is not one. The mirror pair under w ↦ w_0 w w_0 is (A = {3,4,5,7,8,9,11,12,13}, P_z = [[1,2,3,4,8],…]).
There the same U-element appears as the star image s_1·x.

Note: this (P_x, P_z) is the S_14 twin (4,4,3,3) with 15 appended to row 1 of both tableaux. The obstruction y is the
S_14 twin x of (5,3,3,3) with 15 appended to row 2. That x **is** half of an S_15 twin: (P_y, [[1,2,3,7,11],[4,5,6,15],…]) is a TWIN.
So one twin lift sits below the other and kills it.

## 3. The decision for every candidate (`certifyn.py`, `verifyn.py`)

The procedure is PROVE 280 Step 2, re-derived with no row-count shortcut. For x = (P_x, Q_0), the y's that Lemma A
requires are:
- (i) the s·x ∈ Γ with L(sx) ⊄ A, which have μ = 1;
- (ii) the y = (U, Q_0) with Des U ⊋ A and μ(y, x) ≠ 0.

The proof that this list is complete is PROVE 280 Step 2: F1 for y > x and for y < x with A ⊄ L(y), and the remaining
case is exactly (ii).

Each μ that is needed gets a certificate: a path of simultaneous left and/or right stars ending in
- gap 1 and Bruhat-comparable, so μ = 1; or
- even gap, Bruhat-incomparable, or an F1 violation, so μ = 0.

The search is left-only BFS first, then two-sided if needed.
`verifyn.py` replays every certificate with no shared code. It has its own RSK, its own left and right star (asserting
D_L / D_R membership at every step), rank-matrix Bruhat order, and its own enumeration of the y-set in (i)+(ii).
**48/48 verdicts replayed OK** (`out_verify15.txt`).

## 4. Controls

- `control2.py`: the two-sided decision procedure against full KL in S_5 and S_6, over all pairs inside left cells.
  It reproduces 120 + 1118 pairs with μ = 1 and 118 + 2550 with μ = 0. It got 0 wrong and left 0 undecided (`out_control2_S5S6.txt`).
- S_14 re-certified from scratch with the new pipeline in the superstandard cell. That is a different Q from PROVE 280's
  (4,4,3,3) cell. (4,4,3,3) and (4,4,4,1,1) both come out TWIN and verifyn replays them.
- Diagram automorphism w ↦ w_0 w w_0: all 48 verdicts are consistent with it, in 26 orbits (15 twin, 11 no).
  This is a free separator, and it passed.
- Cell independence. `dualconn.py` checks that the right-star graph on the cells of each of the 9 shapes is connected:
  it reaches f^λ of f^λ cells in every case. Right stars biject Γ → Γ′ and preserve L and μ, so every verdict holds in
  **every** left cell of the shape. As a cross-check, `randQ.py` re-decides pairs from scratch in random cells, rebuilding the y-set each time:
  53/53 agree (`out_randQ_*.txt`).

## 5. Persistence and conjugation (data only)

- **Shape level: persistence holds.** Every λ + □ of every S_14 twin shape carries an S_15 twin.
- **Pair level: the naive lift fails.** Appending 15 to the same row of both S_14 twin tableaux gives 12 lifts:
  - 7 TWIN;
  - 1 NO (the U case above: 4433 → 5433 via row 1);
  - 4 not candidates at all, because the new descent at 14 is not interior.

  The twins in 5442, 54411, 53331 and 44331 come from inserting a box in the *middle* block boundary. For example,
  A^c = {3,7,8,12} is a twin and {3,7,11,12} is not.
- **Conjugation.** It is not a symmetry of twins, since w ↦ w w_0 reverses L-inclusion. The twin *shapes* are
  conjugation-closed at n = 14 and n = 15. Pair counts are not: 5433 has 4 twin pairs and 44421 has 6.
  No structural reason is claimed.
- **GW (4,4,1).** 7 of the 9 twin shapes contain (4,4,1); (6,3,3,3) and (5,3,3,3,1) do not (as with (5,3,3,3) at n = 14).
  So "contains (4,4,1)" is not the twin criterion. Twins and GW's Ex 4.21 remain different mechanisms.

## Gaps

1. Lemma S, F1 and P_{a,b} = P_{a⁻¹,b⁻¹} rest on the basic KL79 recursion facts, which are recall-only. This is the same
   status as PROVE 280 gap 1. All three are validated in S_5 and S_6 against full KL.
2. Cell connectivity is a finite exhaustive computation for these 9 shapes. In general it is Haiman's dual-equivalence theorem (recall-only).
3. The revised criterion "twin ⟺ C2 + parity + no U-obstruction" is a **hunch**, with 48/48 consistency at n = 15. It is not even
   precise yet: "no U-obstruction" is just "Lemma A holds at the (ii)-elements", so as stated it is close to a tautology. The
   live question is whether the (ii)-elements always come from another candidate x, as they do here.
4. The shape-level statement "a shape has a twin ⟺ it carries a (★)-violation" survives n = 15 (9/9). It stays a hunch.
